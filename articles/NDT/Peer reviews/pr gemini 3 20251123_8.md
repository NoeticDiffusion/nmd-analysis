### 1. Critical Formal Error: The "Ghost Meta-Field" Paradox

**Issue:**
In the Extended MNDM (Equation 2), the meta-regulatory field $\Phi(t)$ is included solely as an additive term in the potential $F$:
$ F(X, t) = \dots - \delta(t) \Phi(t) $

The drift of the system is defined by the gradient of $F$ with respect to the state $X$:
$ dX_t = - \mathbf{G}_t(X_t) \nabla_X F(X_t, t) dt + \dots $

Since $\Phi(t)$ is a global scalar (a function of time, not state), its gradient with respect to $X$ is zero:
$ \nabla_X (-\delta(t)\Phi(t)) = 0 $

**Consequence:**
Mathematically, $\Phi(t)$ **has zero effect on the trajectory of the noetic state $X_t$**. It shifts the absolute value of the potential energy (and thus the Coherence $\Omega$), but it creates no forces.
The text claims $\Phi$ acts as a "meta-regulatory field" that "adapts based on how well denoising is working" (Line 506) and "reflects a more conservative, stability-biased regime" (Line 552). Under the current equation, these claims are false. The "confidence" variable $\Phi$ is disconnected from the control loop.

**Required Fix:**
To regulate stability, $\Phi$ must modulate the dynamics. Two options:
1.  **Inverse Gain (Restoration with Care):** Reintroduce $\Phi^{-1}$ as a pre-factor to the drift ($-\Phi^{-1} \mathbf{G} \nabla F$), restoring its role as "inertia" or "inverse precision." You previously removed this to avoid a "paradox," but removing it entirely broke the feedback loop. You must accept that high confidence ($\Phi$) = high stability (low drift) = low learning rate.
2.  **Potential Reshaping:** Make $\Phi$ modulate the *curvature* of $F$, not just its offset. e.g., $F = \Phi(t) \cdot (E - R)$. Then $\nabla F \propto \Phi$, and high $\Phi$ steepens the gradients (increasing restoration force), which is a different kind of stability (stiffness vs. damping).

### 2. Learning Rule Stability ($\mathbf{G}_t$)

**Issue:**
The learning rule for the Attention Tensor $\mathbf{G}_t$ (Eq. after line 833) is driven by $\langle \dot{X} \dot{X}^T \rangle$ gated by "success" (negative $dF/dt$).
$ \Delta \mathbf{G} \propto \langle v v^T \rangle_{\text{successful}} $

**Critique:**
Since $\mathbf{G}$ is the *inverse* metric (mobility), increasing it makes the system move faster.
*   Mechanism: The system moves successfully in direction $u$. The rule increases mobility in direction $u$.
*   Feedback: In the next pass, the gradient force produces an even higher velocity in direction $u$ ($v = -\mathbf{G} \nabla F$).
*   Result: The system accelerates indefinitely in successful directions until it hits the regularization boundary or the landscape flattens.
*   **Assessment:** While the regularization term $-\lambda(\mathbf{G} - \mathbf{I})$ prevents explosion, this positive feedback loop ("The more I move, the easier it gets to move") creates a "ballistic" tendency. This might be intended (habit formation), but it risks instability where the system "overshoots" minima because it has learned zero resistance in that path.

### 3. Theoretical Consistency: "Reshaping" vs "Shifting"

**Textual Inconsistency:**
Line 550 states: "$Phi(t)$ ... acts by shifting and **reshaping** $F$ via the $-\delta(t)\Phi(t)$ term".
*   **Correction:** Mathematically, an additive term $C(t)$ *shifts* a function but does *not* reshape it. Reshaping implies changing the location or curvature of minima. The text promises a mechanism (reshaping) that the equation does not deliver.
