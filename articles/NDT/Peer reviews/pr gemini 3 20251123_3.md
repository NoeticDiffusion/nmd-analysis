### 1. Dynamical Stability & The Meta-Field ($\Phi$)

**Issue:** The dynamics of the meta-regulatory field $\Phi$ described in Eq. 2 and the surrounding text create a potential "Lock-in" trap.
*   **Mechanism:** The drift is scaled by $\Phi(t)^{-1}$ (inverse gain), and $\Phi$ increases when "denoising is effective." The text implies effectiveness is measured by low error or stability.
*   **Critique:** If the system settles into a *local minimum* (where $\nabla F \approx 0$), the "effectiveness" signal (e.g., low gradient norm $\langle \nabla F, \mathbf{G} \nabla F \rangle$) might cause $\Phi$ to rise (increasing confidence/stability). This increases the damping ($\Phi^{-1} \to 0$), effectively freezing the system in that local minimum.
*   **Risk:** Without a mechanism for $\Phi$ to sense the *absolute* quality of the state (the value of $F$, not just the gradient $\nabla F$), the system effectively treats any stagnation as success. "Confidence" should arguably drop if $F$ remains high, even if $\nabla F$ is zero.
*   **Recommendation:** Explicitly include the potential value $F(X)$ or the deviation term $D(X)$ in the evolution equation for $\Phi$ ($\mathcal{B}$), ensuring that confidence only rises if the state is both stable *and* high-quality (low energy).

### 2. Geometry Learning Instability

**Issue:** The learning rule for the Attention Tensor $\mathbf{G}_t$ (Eq. after line 620) is Hebbian on velocities: $\Delta \mathbf{G} \propto \langle \dot{X} \dot{X}^T \rangle$.
*   **Critique:** Since $\mathbf{G}$ is defined as the *inverse* metric (mobility/attention), increasing $\mathbf{G}$ along the direction of motion makes the system move *faster* in that direction for the same gradient ($dX \sim \mathbf{G} \nabla F$).
*   **Consequence:** This creates a positive feedback loop. If the system moves fast along dimension $i$, $\mathbf{G}_{ii}$ increases, reducing resistance, causing it to move even faster in the next pass. While the decay term $-\lambda_G(\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{I})$ provides regularization, the core driving force is destabilizing.
*   **Recommendation:** Clarify the timescale separation. If $\mathbf{G}$ evolves much slower than $X$, this might just mean "we learn to attend to features that change quickly." However, consider if the learning rule should be bounded or if it should be based on *successful* gradients (gradients that reduced $F$) rather than raw velocity (which includes noise).

### 3. Terminology: "Phase Transition"

**Issue:** Section "Meaning Crystallization" (Line 488) equates an "Aha!" moment with a "rapid phase transition... abrupt drop in $F$."
*   **Critique:** In dynamical systems, a trajectory falling into a basin is a *transient*, not a phase transition. A phase transition implies a change in the *stability landscape itself* (e.g., a bifurcation where a fixed point appears or disappears), or a collective ordering event in the thermodynamic limit.
*   **Refinement:** Unless you are positing that the *landscape* ($F$) changes abruptly (which would be a bifurcation), it is more accurate to call this "Basin Entry" or "Trajectory Convergence." Using "Phase Transition" loosely invites unnecessary criticism from physicists.

### 4. Empirical Rigor: "Gradient Invariance"

**Issue:** The "Gradient Invariance" requirement (Line 1464) is exceptionally strong: "The direction of flow... should correlate ($r > 0.7$) across chart choices."
*   **Critique:** Different entropy measures (e.g., Spectral vs. Sample) often have non-linear relationships. Expecting their gradients (local slopes) to correlate at $r > 0.7$ in high-dimensional space is a very high bar. If this fails, does it falsify the theory, or just the utility of one of the charts?
*   **Implication:** This commits the theory to the claim that "Noetic Entropy" is a singular, objective quantity that different metrics approximate linearly locally.
*   **Recommendation:** Keep it if you are confident, but be aware this is the easiest point for an empirical rebuttal to attack. A weaker claim (e.g., "preservation of attractor locations") might be safer than "preservation of vector field direction."

### 5. Minor Corrections

*   **Line 340:** The term $\Phi(t)^{-1}$ appears in the equation. Ensure the text definition of $\Phi$ (Line 380: "increases when... effective") matches the math. If $\Phi$ is "confidence," high confidence -> $\Phi$ high -> $\Phi^{-1}$ low -> low drift (stability). This logic holds, provided the "Lock-in" issue (Point 1) is addressed.
*   **Line 703:** "Plasticity rules that depress synapses during rapid state transitions... versus strengthen them during dwell times." This mechanism for Laplacian smoothing is intuitive but heuristic. It would be stronger if framed as "Time-dependent plasticity approximates a diffusion on the weight space."

### Final Verdict

The manuscript has improved significantly in formal precision (e.g., clarifying $\mathbf{G}$ as the inverse metric). The main remaining theoretical risks are the stability of the meta-dynamics (Lock-in effect) and the potentially runaway Hebbian learning for geometry. Addressing Eq. 2's $\Phi$ dynamics to prevent "confident stagnation" is the most critical fix.
