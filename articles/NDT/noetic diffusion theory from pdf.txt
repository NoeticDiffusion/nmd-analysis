7.2. The Noetic Atlas: Geometric and topological tools
The Noetic Atlas applies four complementary analysis streams to MNPS‑embedded data,
each revealing different aspects of denoising dynamics:
Stream 1: Manifold embedding and geodesic flow. Low‑dimensional projection (for
example, PCA, PHATE, Laplacian eigenmaps) reveals intrinsic dimensionality and trajectory
structure [36]. We compute global dimensionality (participation ratio, intrinsic‑dimension

estimates), geodesic distances between states, local curvature (tangent‑space PCA), and flow
velocity and directionality along principal manifold axes.
Stream 2: Topological Data Analysis (Mapper/persistent homology). TDA reveals
the “shape” of explored state space without assuming linearity. Mapper constructs a graph
where nodes are clusters of similar states and edges encode transitions [38], [39], [40]. We extract
hub nodes (high betweenness), component fragmentation (number of disconnected subgraphs),
and topological features (loops, voids) via persistent homology (Betti numbers) [41].
Stream 3: Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA). RQA characterizes how
trajectories revisit previous states. From recurrence plots we extract recurrence rate (RR),
determinism (DET), laminarity (LAM), and entropy of line‑length distributions (structural
complexity of revisits) [42].
Stream 4: Metastability and criticality indices. These quantify proximity to the critical
regime where denoising is maximally informative: metastability index (variance of synchrony
over time), avalanche‑like branching‑ratio estimates, and DFA exponents capturing long‑range
temporal correlations.
Each stream provides complementary information: Embedding reveals trajectory paths,
TDA reveals manifold structure, RQA reveals temporal organization, and metastability indices
quantify proximity to criticality. Together, they form a comprehensive geometric portrait of
denoising dynamics.
8. Noetic Diffusion Health Index (NDHI)
NDHI aggregates manifold dimensionality, curvature smoothness, hub centrality/connector
reliability, recurrence structure, and metastability into a single score. Crucially, these compo
nents are treated as deviations from a healthy optimum (inverted U profile) or distance from a
reference centroid, not simple maximizations. This prevents pathological extremes (e.g., hyper
synchrony, which would maximize “smoothness” but destroy information) from scoring highly. It
serves as a summary of manifold “fitness” across states and subjects, grounded in evidence that
near critical and metastable regimes optimize information processing and flexibility [43], [44].
Components draw on reliable metrics of synchrony/metastability [45] and critical‑like dynamics
[46], and we predict NDHI correlates with cognition via proximity to criticality [47].
In this version of the work, NDHI is used as a Stage 1 summary index and tool for cross
condition visualization rather than as a finalized clinical biomarker; its exact component weights
and thresholds are expected to be refined as empirical validation accumulates.
Throughout, terms such as “health” and “fitness” are meant strictly operationally: they
refer to proximity to a metastable/near critical regime defined by these dynamical metrics, not
to broader normative or value laden judgements beyond those functional criteria.
Weighting rationale and alternatives. We use equal weights initially to preserve
interpretability and avoid overfitting; each component captures a distinct facet (dimension,
curvature, topology, recurrence, metastability). As a robustness check, we report sensitivity to
±50 % weight perturbations and can optionally compute cross‑validated linear weights solely for
predictive benchmarking. Learned‑weight variants are reported as exploratory sensitivity
analyses and are not the primary biomarker, in order to maintain construct validity.

9. Predictions and falsifiable claims
NDT makes specific, testable predictions that distinguish it from alternative frameworks.
We organize these by type: signature predictions (novel patterns NDT uniquely predicts),
competitive predictions (where NDT and alternatives make different predictions), and
falsification criteria (observations that would disprove core claims).
Effect size priors and thresholds. Where we quote specific effect sizes (for example,
Δ𝐸 ≈ 20–35 % or 𝑑 > 0.6), these are grounded in a combination of (i) literature based priors
—especially recent syntheses on SO–spindle mechanisms and sleep dependent entropy changes
(for example, [28])—and (ii) simulation results using the minimal MNDM that indicate the scale
at which predicted topology/entropy signatures become reliably detectable under realistic noise
and sample sizes. We therefore treat thresholds such as 𝑑 ≥ 0.6 or 𝑟 ≥ 0.3 as conservative
targets for “strong support”, while smaller but consistent effects are still informative as
“moderate” or “exploratory” evidence, provided they meet the preregistered robustness criteria
described later in the methods section.

events
Claim. During NREM sleep, neural entropy should decline in discrete steps time‑locked
to SO–spindle (and ripple) events, not smoothly.
Operationalization. Detect SO–spindle complexes in sleep EEG; compute spectral/sample

Predicted pattern. Entropy drops Δ𝐸 ≈ 15–30 % within one spindle duration (∼0.5–1.5 s)

overnight entropy reduction (𝑟 ∼ 0.3–0.5), (ii) next‑day memory performance (𝑟 ∼ 0.2–0.4), (iii)

9.7. Failure modes, confounds, and unfalsifiability patterns
We enumerate concrete failure modes and how the preregistered analysis circumvents unfal
sifiability:
• Chart‑flexibility inflation: Post‑hoc switching of MNPS features (𝐸, 𝑅, 𝐷) could manu
facture effects. Guardrail: Predefine a multiverse grid and require directional consistency
across >60 % of specifications with FDR control; report Procrustes alignment between MNPS
variants (target 𝑅2 > 0.7).
• Schedule over‑flexibility: Allowing 𝑟(𝑡) to be freely re‑fit risks tautology. Guardrail:
Construct ̂ 𝑟(𝑡) from observables with fixed methods and cross‑validated weights; treat 𝜎(𝑡)
amplitude as the only free scalar in Stage 1 estimation.
• Event‑detection circularity: Using entropy changes to define events would guarantee
step‑like drops. Guardrail: Detect SO/spindles with canonical band‑limited criteria and
circular statistics independent of entropy, then test entropy time‑locked to those events.
• Parameter overfitting: Rich parameterizations (for example, adaptive 𝑮𝑡) may fit noise.
Guardrail: Nested model comparison with BIC and held‑out prediction; accept added
complexity only with Δ BIC > 10 and >10 % test‑set error reduction.
• Hidden confounds: Vigilance, motion, and medication can mimic topology changes.
Guardrail: Regress standardized confounds, analyze within‑subject stage contrasts, and
verify absence of effects in phase‑randomized surrogates.
• Unfalsifiability pattern A (moving targets): Redefining predictions after seeing data.
Guardrail: Preregister operationalizations, parameter grids, and success thresholds.

12. Related work
We situate NDT relative to several major frameworks:
• Predictive coding / Active Inference: NDT shares the focus on probabilistic inference
and precision but emphasizes rhythmic scheduling and event‑locked denoising steps rather
than purely smooth precision dynamics.
• Criticality and metastability: Prior work characterizes near‑critical regimes; NDT adds
a concrete mechanism (RVC/TRN‑VG) for actively steering the system near this edge and
makes topology‑level predictions.
• ML diffusion models: MNDM borrows reverse‑time denoising intuition but constrains
schedules to endogenous rhythms and distributes the “score” over biological circuits, yielding
predictions about lesions, perturbations, and adaptive geometry.
• Embodied/interoceptive inference: EAP aligns with these accounts but adds an
explicitly rhythmic, phase‑structured self‑prior that modulates both landscape and variance
schedules.
• Geometric/TDA approaches: The Noetic Atlas builds on manifold and TDA methods
but is driven by a generative dynamical model (MNDM) rather than remaining purely
descriptive.
13. Limitations and future work
We explicitly acknowledge key limitations:
• Learning problem: MNDM specifies dynamics given 𝐹 and 𝑮𝑡 but does not yet derive
how they are learned from synaptic/circuit plasticity; the tri‑phase learning architecture
is a proposal needing empirical tests. A complementary, more speculative subcellular
implementation—in which parts of the noetic metric 𝑮𝑡 are instantiated by microtubular
“key–lock” gating in dendrites—is developed in a companion paper on Microtubular Key–
Lock Geometry [51]; that work should be read as one possible realization pathway rather
than a prerequisite for the present MNDM formalism.
• Measurement and identifiability: Coordinate choices for MNPS introduce ambiguity;
robust multiverse analysis and targeted perturbations are needed to disambiguate models.
A tensorial extension that focuses specifically on second order dynamics in MNPS—the
Meta‑Noetic Jacobian (MNJ), capturing how the rules of change vary locally—is developed
in a separate theoretical paper [52]; the present work only uses first order MNPS trajectories
but is constructed so that MNJ style analyses can be layered on top of the same coordinates
and pipelines.
• Phenomenological methods: Claims about pulsed experience, presence, and embodiment
require high‑resolution neurophenomenology that is still under development.

Hard‑problem scope: NDT addresses when/how denoising unfolds and what geometry
it leaves, not why denoising is accompanied by subjective experience.
Future work should unify score‑learning with predictive‑error plasticity, incorporate action and
active inference, model development and lifespan changes in schedules, extend to multi‑agent
settings, and explore laminar implementations and geometric pharmacology in detail.
14. Conclusion
We summarize the thesis that conscious reconstruction is rhythmic, step‑wise denoising
on a learned manifold, highlight three core advances (discrete timing via RVC/TRN‑VG,
geometry‑first visibility via the Atlas/NDHI, and embodied conditioning via EAP), and sketch
implications for AI, clinical practice, and philosophy. NDT is offered as a research program with
concrete next steps (public‑dataset analyses, closed‑loop perturbation experiments, geometric
pharmacology) and an invitation to treat manifold health as a measurable, cultivable aspect
of mind.
