The Meta–Noetic Diffusion Model (MNDM) formalizes this process as a guided stochastic differential equation with a rhythmic variance schedule, and Rhythmic Variance Control (RVC) links that schedule to measurable neural rhythms. The core empirical target is not a complete theory of subjective experience, but a constrained set of dynamical signatures that can be tested in neural recordings.To make the framework measurable, we define the Meta–Noetic Phase Space (MNPS) as the canonical Stage-1 coordinate system; optional Noetic Atlas readouts are specified in Appendix Section 16.2. The first-stage analysis focuses on three primary claims: event-locked entropy changes around slow-oscillation–spindle complexes, sleep-stage transformations in MNPS topology (NREM centralization and REM expansion), and bounded disorder-related geometry contrasts that must survive preregistered multiverse controls. A composite Noetic Diffusion Health Index (NDHI) is retained only as an exploratory visualization and summary index, not as a clinical biomarker or consciousness score.The article therefore offers a staged research program: a minimal dynamical model, a reproducible measurement layer, explicit null models, and falsifiable public-dataset tests. Speculative extensions—embodied anchoring, detailed TRN biophysics, landscape learning, second-order Jacobian analyses, reachability, and clinical translation—are treated as appendices or follow-on work rather than prereq­uisites for the core NDT claim.1.Scope and Epistemic StatusNDT provides a mechanistic framework for neural dynamics associated with conscious-state reports, but we carefully distinguish three levels of claim:Strong claims (empirically testable):•Neural rhythms (theta–gamma PAC, SO–spindle coupling) implement variance scheduling that produces measurable entropy dynamics.•Manifold topology in Meta–Noetic Phase Space (MNPS) systematically varies with sleep stage, task context, and some clinical contrasts.•Null models without event-locked variance scheduling should fail to reproduce the strongest rhyth­mic and topological signatures.Moderate claims (hypothesized correlations):•These neural geometric signatures correlate with reported conscious states (presence, clarity, embod­iment).•First-person phenomenology is hypothesized to exhibit structure (pulsed quality, discrete epochs) aligned with neural rhythmic events.•Clinical groups may show characteristic manifold deformations, but these are treated as bounded, dataset-specific hypotheses rather than diagnostic categories.Speculative claims (philosophical):•The geometric flow described by MNDM may be constitutive of consciousness (experience is the intrinsic character of rhythmic denoising).•This constitutive hypothesis does not generate empirical predictions beyond those of the correlational dynamical model; it is a metaphysical stance on what the already-specified denoising dynamics amount to. All concrete predictions in this manuscript bear on the presence or absence of the denoising/geometric signatures themselves; if those are supported, one may choose to read them either in a purely correlational or in a more constitutive spirit, but no behavioural or neural test uniquely adjudicates between these readings.Extension-status rule. Later NDT work includes synthetic recoverability audits, subsystem hypothe­ses, and measurement-layer companion analyses. These have distinct evidentiary roles: a truth-known simulator can validate an estimator under its frozen synthetic contract without validating an empirical MNJ; an observational body–brain association can constrain EAP without identifying causal entrain­ment; and a circuit proposal remains a hypothesis until its own causal test succeeds. We do not promote any of these extensions to the Stage-1 claim set by inclusion in this article.Throughout, we use “conscious state reconstruction” as shorthand for “neural dynamics exhibiting systematic correlates with conscious states.” The hard problem of why any neural process is accom­panied by subjective experience remains unaddressed.2.IntroductionThis paper introduces a formal, testable account of how neural systems may rhythmically alternate between exploration and denoising, and how those dynamics become visible as geometric structure in neural state space. We use Noetic Diffusion Theory (NDT) as the scientific umbrella, the Meta–Noetic Diffusion Model (MNDM) as the mathematical core, and the Meta–Noetic Phase Space (MNPS) as the measurement layer. Here, “noetic” denotes knowledge-bearing or intelligible structure: latent neural states whose organization can be measured in a principled way. Our framing complements predictive coding/active inference perspectives [1], [2] and draws on neurophilosophical accounts of state-space representation [3], [4], [5] and real patterns [6].When you wake from a dream, the world reconstructs itself in seconds. Chaotic imagery resolves into your bedroom; formless unease crystallizes into the memory of today’s dead­line. This transition—from noise to meaning—is neither instantaneous nor continuous, but iterative, rhythmic, and geometrically structured.The motivating phenomenological intuition is that some conscious episodes may feel like progressive reconstruction: vague, noisy structure becomes a usable world model over short time windows. This intuition motivates the language of denoising, but the core tests in this manuscript are third-person dynamical tests: event-locked entropy, MNPS topology, recurrence, and model comparison against non-rhythmic nulls. First-person anchoring, embodiment, and high- resolution neurophenomenology are treated as Tier-2 extensions rather than requirements for the initial validation of NDT.Reading guide. The brain maintains a noetic state 𝑋 that balances exploration (maintaining uncertainty to sample alternatives, consider possibilities) against coherence (collapsing uncertainty into stable, actionable meaning). Too much exploration yields confusion; too much coherence yields rigidity. Rhythms set when to explore vs. cohere, implementing a dynamic schedule that navigates this trade-off. The MNDM formalizes this as a guided diffusion on a learned potential landscape 𝐹 (acquired through development and experience): trajectories descend where meaning is strong, pause or re-explore when rhythms loosen variance, and re-stabilize when variance tightens. Healthy cognition requires 𝑟(𝑡) to vary, alternating denoising and exploration.3.1.1.Note on pathological extremes (the Grand Mal boundary)A critical limitation of the minimal potential 𝐹min=𝑒−𝑚 is that it can mistake low entropy plus high rhythmic structure for successful denoising. Taken to its logical extreme, a generalized tonic-clonic seizure—with hypersynchronous high-amplitude oscillations and reduced signal complexity—could look like a maximally coherent state. This is phenomenologically false and clinically absurd.The resolution is to treat the minimal model as a physiological-corridor approximation, not as a complete regime classifier. As Churchland (1986, 1995) emphasizes, awareness requires functional differentiation as well as integration [3], [5]. Later NDT work makes the same point operationally: reduced capacity must be decomposed into capacity collapse, transport-innovation balance, and patho­logical lock-in rather than collapsed into a single scalar [10]. A seizure-like regime is not merely “low entropy”; it is transport-captured, canalized, and pathologically locked.The extended potential therefore includes the deviation term 𝑑(𝑋) (Eq. 7), which penalizes depar­tures from an integration-segregation corridor. The minimal formulation (Eq. 1) assumes uniform attention across the noetic manifold and fixed balance between entropy and coherence. But the brain clearly modulates what to denoise (attention) and when different pressures dominate (varying schedules).We hypothesize that the neural correlates of consciousness are orchestrated by multiple coordi­nated schedules operating at different timescales—variance 𝜎(𝑡) setting the tempo, landscape weights 𝛼,𝛽,𝛾,𝛿 composing the score, geometry 𝑮𝑡 directing attention, and embodied anchoring providing the somatic foundation. The extended system adds four ingredients to the minimal state update: a geometry tensor that reshapes the descent direction, an adaptation rule for that geometry, an adaptation rule for the meta-field, and a richer, time-varying potential. The practical rule is hierarchical: start with Minimal‑MNDM, add complexity only if simpler rhythm and null models fail under held-out prediction and preregistered robustness criteria.3.7.Neurophenomenology as a Tier-2 extensionNDT is motivated by the possibility that first-person reports of clarity, presence, and temporal organi­zation covary with neural denoising dynamics. However, current experience-sampling methods are generally too slow and noisy to adjudicate sub-second claims about pulsed experience. We therefore treat neurophenomenological links as Tier-2: useful for future validation, but not part of the Stage-1 evidence required for the core MNDM/RVC/MNPS tests.The immediate empirical program uses third-person signatures only: event-locked entropy, rhythm proxies, MNPS topology, recurrence, and null-model comparison. Claims about embodiment, pres­ence, insight, depersonalization, or self-prior strength are reserved for appendix-level EAP work and later studies with direct first-person measurement.3.8.Landscape learning as a follow-on moduleThe MNDM specifies denoising dynamics given a potential landscape 𝐹(𝑋,𝑡) and, in the extended model, an attention geometry 𝑮𝑡. How those structures are acquired is an important but separate question. In this core article we therefore treat landscape learning as a follow-on module, not as a prerequisite for testing Minimal-MNDM, RVC, or MNPS signatures.A plausible extension is that wake prediction errors sculpt local basins, NREM replay stabilizes task-relevant regions, and REM-like high-variance periods regularize over-sharp minima. These ideas motivate later experiments on replay, consolidation, and development, but the Tier-1 tests below require only the observable trajectory, rhythm proxy 𝑟̂(𝑡), and MNPS features. Failure of a specific wake/NREM/REM learning rule would therefore revise the learning module without falsifying the core rhythmic-geometry claim.Detailed learning equations, replay kernels, and developmental hypotheses are best handled in dedicated follow-up manuscripts or appendix material once the Stage-1 measurement contract has been validated across public datasets.The Hippocampal–Entorhinal Landscape System (HELS) is one such prospective contact point: replay and history may update a context-conditioned landscape 𝐹𝑐, with primary future endpoints defined as later transition probabilities, dwell, corridor reachability, or hysteresis rather than a mean shift in (𝑚,𝑑,𝑒). Current HELS work validates analysis infrastructure and eligibility contracts, not a landscape-writing mechanism. We estimate 𝑟(𝑡) from EEG/MEG/LFP using validated methods: (i) phase–amplitude coupling via modulation index or mean vector length (Tort et al., 2010; see also [15], [20]); (ii) SO/spindle detection via amplitude thresholding or wavelet transforms, with phase precision quantified by circular statistics (for example, Mölle et al., 2011); (iii) cross-regional phase coherence via weighted phase lag index (wPLI) or phase-locking value (PLV); and (iv) coupling within SO–spindle–ripple coordination in intracranial and scalp data [21], [22], [23].Operational definitions and multiple-comparisons control. For event-locked analyses we use pre-registered windows (baseline: [−1.0,0] s; post-event: [0,1.5] s relative to SO–spindle onset), sliding-window entropy (500 ms, 50 % overlap), and alignment latencies constrained to biologically plausible ranges (spindle onset within ±200 ms of SO up-state). Detection thresholds follow literature priors (SO: 0.5–1 Hz band, amplitude >75th percentile; spindles: 11–16 Hz, duration 0.5–2 s). Family-wise error is controlled with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across channels and parameter multiverse. Effect-size priors from recent syntheses (for example, [24]) set expectations of Δ𝑒≈15–30 % and medium effects (𝑑∼0.4–0.6) for coupled events.For step-wise entropy analysis: Compute spectral/sample entropy in sliding windows (for example, 500 ms), detect SO–spindle or theta peaks, align entropy time series to these events, and test for systematic drops time-locked to events (permutation testing against phase-shuffled controls). Key prediction: Entropy should drop after event onset with timing that scales with event duration (longer spindles → deeper/longer entropy reduction).Perturbation strategy. Closed-loop stimulation provides causal tests: (i) tACS at theta or SO frequencies can enhance or disrupt natural coupling; (ii) stimulation at anti-phase (for example, 180° relative to detected SO) should specifically disrupt temporal ordering of denoising steps (NDT predicts items encoded during anti-phase show false temporal associations), while in-phase stimulation enhances them; (iii) in animals, optogenetic manipulation of TRN sectors tests whether variance control is necessary and sufficient for the predicted geometric changes.Failure modes and perturbation roadmap. Schedule dissonance—weak, desynchronized, or pathologically rigid rhythm control—should alter recurrence, connectors, and manifold accessibility. Pharmacological and stimulation studies may eventually test this causal chain, but such intervention claims are Tier‑2 and require dedicated safety, dosing, and preregistration. In this core article they serve only as a roadmap for future perturbation work, with readouts at the level of component readouts rather than a single health score.Falsifiable contrasts. If RVC genuinely implements discrete steps, entropy declines should be step-wise and event-locked; anti-phase disruption should scramble order (temporal misbinding), not just reduce overall precision. Smooth, monotonic entropy changes and insensitivity to timing would falsify this mechanism.Embodied Anchoring Principle (EAP; Tier‑2 extension). EAP treats denoising as conditioned on interoceptive state via a self‑prior and body‑synchronized variance gating. Guardrail: Regress standardized confounds, analyze within‑subject stage contrasts, and verify absence of effects in phase‑randomized surrogates.•Unfalsifiability pattern A (moving targets): Redefining predictions after seeing data. Guardrail: Preregister operationalizations, parameter grids, and success thresholds.•Unfalsifiability pattern B (equivalence classes): Claiming success under transformations that preserve ordering. Guardrail: Use interventions that break equivalences (anti‑phase rhythmic stim­ulation; TRN‑targeted pharmacology) and require order‑specific effects.In addition, we include null‑model controls (random‑walk trajectories and continuous‑precision models) alongside Minimal‑MNDM in simulations and empirical fits; step‑wise, event‑locked entropy drops and rhythm‑locked topology changes must exceed these nulls under identical preprocessing and FDR control.7.Simulated demonstrationsTo demonstrate that the MNDM can reproduce key theoretical predictions, we simulate the minimal formulation (Eq. 1) under several scenarios capturing core dynamics: wake‑to‑sleep transitions, sleep cycling with curriculum structure, and disorder‑typical perturbations. A simulator-first candidate mechanism—replay-depen­dent slow branch-level accessibility—reproduces a joint linking/perturbation/rescue signature not matched by nine tested simpler comparators [34], and a follow-on truth-known observability audit separately maps which measurement objects can and cannot recover that mechanism under partial, non-oracle observation (Appendix Section 16.4) [35]. Both remain synthetic model-discrimination and measurement-recoverability results, not validated biological plasticity or a fitted NDT learning rule; the tri‑phase learning architecture and HELS remain the open bridge to direct, content-specific transition or reachability tests on real circuits. Existing HELS analyses validate infrastructure, not a mechanism.•Measurement and identifiability: Coordinate choices for MNPS introduce ambiguity; robust multiverse analysis and targeted perturbations are needed to disambiguate models (see below).•Phenomenological methods: Claims about pulsed experience, presence, and embodiment require high‑resolution neurophenomenology that is still under development. Operational content sections and fibre language (Appendix Section 16.6) organize hypotheses, and a dedicated measurement-precursor program has now started: in mouse visual cortex, population decoders trained only on real, orientation-matched edges generalize to illusory-contour trials beyond shuffled-label and region-specificity controls. This is a genuinely blind, frozen content-generalization decoding result, not a phenomenal-report measurement; it neither measures qualia nor closes the hard problem.•Clinical translation: Geometric pharmacology and intervention design are explicitly future‑work goals; any clinical pathway claims are provisional until replicated with preregistered, dataset‑specific validation.•Hard‑problem scope: NDT addresses when/how denoising unfolds and what geometry it leaves, not why denoising is accompanied by subjective experience. This is a permanent scope boundary: no measurement-recoverability audit or content-decoding result above is intended or able to close it.9.1.Identifiability of landscape, geometry, and scheduleAt the level of observables, (𝐹,𝑮𝑡,𝑟(𝑡)) are partially non-identifiable: different combinations of potential shape, attention geometry, and variance schedule can yield similar trajectories in MNPS. Our strategy is threefold. First, chart-level ambiguity is addressed by the multiverse guardrails above, which require that qualitative patterns (for example, step‑wise NREM entropy drops, depression‑like manifold collapse) be consistent across many reasonable (𝑚,𝑑,𝑒) definitions. Second, we rely on targeted perturbations that preferentially modulate one component: TRN-focused manipulations can test candidate circuit contributions but cannot be presumed to identify 𝜎(𝑡); attentional tasks and fronto‑parietal lesions should primarily affect 𝑮𝑡; and interventions on plasticity or replay should reshape 𝐹 itself. Third, we explicitly compare Minimal‑MNDM against null models (for example, continuous‑precision “Null B”) in both simulations and data: patterns such as discrete, event‑locked entropy steps or tightly coupled topology changes must be reproducible by Minimal‑MNDM but not by plausible precision‑only models if they are to count as evidence specifically for rhythmic, geometry‑coupled denoising.Future work should unify score‑learning with predictive‑error plasticity, incorporate action and active inference, model development and lifespan changes in schedules, extend to multi‑agent settings, and explore laminar implementations in detail.10.ConclusionWe summarize the thesis that conscious-state correlates may be studied as rhythmic, step‑wise denoising on a learned manifold. The core advances are discrete timing via RVC, a minimal MNDM that can be compared against nulls, and geometry‑first visibility through MNPS and the appendix Noetic Atlas. NDT is offered as a staged research program with concrete next steps: public‑dataset analyses, stricter multiverse controls, and later perturbation experiments once the Stage‑1 measurement layer is stable.11.AcknowledgmentsThis work emerged through structured correspondence among human and AI collaborators; large language models (GPT‑5, Claude, Gemini, Grok) assisted in literature synthesis, drafting, and editorial refinement.12.ContactRobin Langell — hello(at)noeticdiffusion.com13.LicensingThis manuscript provides only a brief licensing summary; full terms and conditions are available via the Mychainos multi‑layer licensing framework (https://mychainos.org/licence/). Neither is a Stage-1 result; HELS currently has validated infrastructure but no validated mechanism result, and CCTG must earn support through dedicated reversal/causal tests. Full equations and status are given in Supplementary Methods S2.16.6.NDT-Q: operational content sectionsMNPS characterizes a global dynamical regime, not the identity of an experienced or represented con­tent.
