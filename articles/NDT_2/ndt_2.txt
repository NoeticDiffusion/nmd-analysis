Noetic Diffusion Theory: A Meso-to-Macro Geometry of Neural State ReconstructionA bottom-up, rhythmically gated framework with explicit measurement boundariesArticle type: Research Article — theoretical frameworkShort title: Noetic Diffusion Theory from meso to macroKeywords: consciousness, neural dynamics, cross-scale coupling, rhythmic variance control, neural manifolds, metabolic constraintRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenFor correspondence: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comAbstractNoetic Diffusion Theory (NDT) proposes that neural-state reconstruction can be studied as rhyth­mically gated stochastic flow on a measured state-space chart. This first meso-to-macro version separates a proposed mechanism from the strength of evidence available for observing it. At the mesoscopic scale, thalamocortical gating and population activity provide candidate implementation and measurement layers; at the macroscopic scale, EEG/MEG/fMRI features define a Meta–Noetic Phase Space (MNPS) chart. A companion Neuropixels–surface-EEG analysis establishes bounded meso–macro associations under temporal and statistical nulls, but its EEG-only local-linear chart does not support a Jacobian or reachability inference. A truth-known simulator further distinguishes association, mechanism discrimination, latent-order preservation, and dynamical-operator identifi­cation. We use these boundaries to formulate a minimal rhythmic diffusion model, an embodied anchoring extension, and a slow metabolic-adequacy gate. The framework predicts observable rhythm-locked changes in state-space geometry while rejecting the inference that any single coordinate, rhythm, or cross-scale correlation measures consciousness. The proposed equations and biological mappings remain hypotheses to be tested against simpler alternatives.Author SummaryNDT describes a possible way that neural rhythms could alternate between exploration and stabilisation. This version begins with local population signals and their measured relationship to EEG before introducing whole-brain geometry. It distinguishes what cross-scale measurements show from stronger claims about mechanisms, latent states, or consciousness, and treats metabolic and bodily signals as constraints on the proposed dynamics rather than as consciousness meters.1.Scope and epistemic statusThis article advances NDT as a theory and measurement framework, not as a completed theory of consciousness. The central empirical object is a measured neural trajectory, whereas the proposed mechanism concerns an unobserved, multiscale process generating that trajectory. The distinction is consequential: an MNPS coordinate is not a consciousness measurement; a cross-scale association does not identify a mechanism; and the metabolic equations below are proposals for testing, not established physiology.We use four evidence labels throughout. Established external results are findings supported outside this programme. Companion empirical or methodological results are auditable analyses that constrain the framework but are not independent validation of it. Plausible interpretations are readings compatible with present evidence. Speculative extensions include the precise MNDM, TRN, and metabolic-gating mechanisms. This structure prevents an attractive analogy from being promoted into a biological result.2.IntroductionNeural dynamics are measured at nested but non-equivalent scales. Local spiking and field potentials, surface electrophysiology, imaging signals, bodily physiology, and subjective report cannot be assumed to be inter­changeable descriptions of one state. A theory that begins with a low-dimensional EEG or fMRI chart must therefore explain what supports that chart, what it omits, and what inferential burden its downstream geometry can bear.This version builds the argument upward. It begins with metabolic availability and candidate mesoscopic variance gating, then asks whether population-scale features can be bridged to surface EEG under strict null tests. Only after these constraints are in place does it introduce MNDM, RVC, MNPS, and embodied anchoring. The intended contribution is not that this chain has been demonstrated end-to-end. Rather, it is a concrete route by which each link can be tested, revised, or rejected.Core questionWhich mesoscopic mechanisms and measurement conditions could support a falsifiable meso-to-macro account of rhythmically gated neural-state reconstruction, without mistaking observable association for a unique latent geometry or a theory of consciousness?3.Scale ladder and observability ruler3.1.From latent process to measured chartLet 𝑋𝑡 denote a latent neural state, 𝑌𝑡 a measurement, Ψ a modality-specific observation map, and 𝐶 a feature/chart construction:𝑌𝑡=𝑔(𝑋𝑡)+𝜀𝑡,𝑧𝑡=𝐵(𝑋𝑡)=𝐶(Ψ(𝑋𝑡))Equation Equation 0 separates the target of inference from its measurement. Some propose a constituent of experience, some specify access or representational conditions, and others describe state transitions or their measurement. NDT is situated in the latter category: a rhythm-sensitive neural-dynamics and measurement framework. It is not, in its present form, an identity theory of consciousness or a model of report, meta-representation, or global broadcast. This layered reading is compatible with integrative multiscale accounts that treat leading theories as partly complementary rather than simple rivals (Storm et al., 2024).The comparison must distinguish conscious level, momentary content, access and report, meta-awareness, bodily selfhood, and dynamical capacity. A state-level discriminator need not identify experience at a moment, and NDT cannot select a philosophical theory of experience merely by supplying a measurement surface for other theories.Figure 4:  Conceptual explanatory-layer map. Its specific, falsifiable addition is the proposed rhythmic schedule: variability should change at predicted events or phases in a way that explains trajectories beyond stationary dynamics, spectral power, generic criticality, and unconstrained state-space models. This claim requires four jointly specified tests:1.Event specificity: changes occur at the predicted event or phase.2.Schedule specificity: an event-locked variance model outperforms stationary-noise and smooth-control alternatives.3.Incremental value: the schedule predicts future trajectory change beyond power, event density, autocorre­lation, complexity, and criticality.4.Null specificity: effects fail under phase-shuffle, time-shift, wrong-event, wrong-subject, and power-matched controls.The appropriate next comparison is adversarial rather than rhetorical. A preregistered multimodal altered-state design should separate physiological state, responsiveness, access, and reported experience; then ask whether NDT’s event-locked schedule adds prediction beyond theory-relevant alternatives. The recent adversarial IIT–GNWT collaboration illustrates the standard: a theory gains scientific force by exposing a specified prediction to possible failure (Cogitate Consortium et al., 2025).12.Predictions and falsification12.1.Tier-1: measurement and rhythmic-dynamics testsThe first prediction concerns the bridge itself. Meso–macro feature associations should survive prespecified temporal and statistical nulls, including time shifts that preserve each signal’s marginal structure. Even when they do, they remain Level-1 results unless an experiment demonstrates mechanism discrimination or stronger recoverability.The second prediction is the core rhythmic claim. Event-locked changes in entropy and trajectory geometry should outperform matched smooth-precision and rhythm-free models in held-out prediction. The relevant outcome is not a descriptive difference between sleep stages, but incremental prediction of future trajectory change after accounting for power, event density, and autocorrelation.The third prediction concerns local dynamics. A chart that supports a Jacobian or reachability interpretation must pass residual-quality, perturbation, and response-generating-operator checks. Failure of any one of these checks limits the result to lower-level association or descriptive geometry.12.2.Tier-2: embodied and metabolic testsThe metabolic extension predicts that hypoglycaemic strain can increase low-frequency rhythmic structure while reducing complexity, effective dimensionality, or reachability. It further predicts that represented metabolic warning and metabolic adequacy can dissociate, particularly where awareness of hypoglycaemia is impaired. Recovery need not retrace induction because metabolic, autonomic, and neural processes can have different time constants. These are Tier-2 hypotheses pending comparison with glucose-as-covariate, autonomic-arousal, and potential-only alternatives.12.3.Falsification criteriaNDT’s rhythmic mechanism is narrowed or rejected if no reproducible event-locked advantage remains after smooth and rhythm-free nulls; if cross-scale associations fail temporal specificity or extraction sensitivity; if local operators fail residual-quality, response-block, or perturbation validation; or if a simpler covariate, arousal, or autonomic model explains the effects equally well or better. These outcomes would not imply that neural geometry is uninformative; they would reject the specific rhythmic-gating interpretation.13.Discussion13.1.ContributionThe contribution is a scale-aware research programme rather than a completed theory of consciousness. The meso–macro bridge constrains what macro measurements may be asked to establish, while the recoverability ladder prevents association, latent ordering, and local dynamics from being collapsed into one claim. The framework’s practical value lies in making its dependencies inspectable: each mechanism, proxy, and derived metric carries a distinct failure condition.13.2.Limitations and next testsNo single dataset currently bridges metabolic state, TRN activity, mesoscopic population dynamics, macro recordings, and first-person report. DANDI 000458 is a stimulation-aligned mouse measurement bridge, not a consciousness experiment. The metabolic equations, TRN mapping, and EAP coupling remain speculative until dedicated studies compare them with simpler alternatives. The next tests should be preregistered cross-scale replications, multimodal estimator-quality validation, controlled metabolic EEG studies, and perturbational rhythm experiments that separate state, access, responsiveness, and reported experience.13.3.ConclusionNDT is most defensible as a falsifiable proposal about rhythmically gated neural state dynamics measured through imperfect, scale-specific charts. The proposed meso-to-macro chain makes its dependencies explicit: metabolic adequacy and population mechanisms may constrain observable rhythms; cross-scale association may justify a measurement bridge; and only validated local dynamics can justify stronger geometry and reachability claims. This is a narrower but more testable contribution than claiming that a particular neural coordinate constitutes consciousness.14.Supplement allocation•S1 Supplementary Methods: full metabolic state, TRN calibration, preprocessing/feature contracts, MNPS algorithms, and estimator diagnostics.•S2 Supplementary Data: companion-analysis tables, parameter ranges, and provenance mappings.•S3 Supplementary QC: temporal nulls, feature multiverse, residual-quality, perturbation, and leave-one-out checks.•S4 Supplementary Clinical: bounded clinical interpretation; no biomarker or individual diagnostic claim.•S5 Glossary: MNDM, RVC, MNPS, observability levels, metabolic adequacy, and reachability.•S6 Changelog: theory-version and claim-ledger changes.15.AcknowledgementsThis manuscript builds on companion meso–macro and truth-known-simulator analyses, and on the metabolic-anchoring research plan.
