Sleep Spindles Phase-Lock to Respiration at Their Ampli­tude Peak, Not Onset, in Human NREM SleepA corrected permutation reanalysis of a companion study's full spindle catalog recovers a robust respiratory coupling missed by an onset-referenced test, with a fragile cardiac counterpart and a weak secondary link to spindle-locked geometryArticle type: Research ArticleShort title: Spindle-peak, not onset, phase-locks to respirationKeywords: sleep spindles, respiratory phase-locking, cardiac phase-locking, circular statistics, embodied anchoring, rhythmic variance controlRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenFor correspondence: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comAbstractA companion three-dataset replication study (RVC_SLEEP) reported a null result for spindle-onset cardiac/respiratory phase-locking in human NREM sleep, using a two-stage hierarchical Rayleigh test on a 190-event, geometry-valid RichSleep subsample. An independent theoretical review of that result flagged the subsample as underpowered and recommended a pseudo-replication-safe permutation reanalysis on RichSleep’s full raw spindle catalog rather than the geometry-filtered subsample. We reanalyzed all 23,029 detected RichSleep spindles (24 subjects) using a subject-specific random-phase-rotation permutation null and a first+second-harmonic omnibus statistic. Spindle-onset phase-locking remained null for both cardiac and respiratory channels at roughly 120× the original sample size (all BH-FDR 𝑞≥0.48). However, spindle-peak (amplitude-envelope-maximum) phase showed a robust, population-level respiratory coupling (pooled 𝑅1=0.045, permutation 𝑝=0.0005; second-order 𝑅=0.665 across 24 subjects, 𝑝=7×10−6) and a weaker, less robust cardiac coupling (pooled 𝑅1=0.016, 𝑝=0.02; second-order 𝑅=0.462, 𝑝=0.005). Four independent robustness checks - duration-tercile stratification, leave-one-subject-out jackknife, deterministic split-half replication, and phase-convention calibration against the export’s documented convention - confirmed the respiratory effect holds in every subsample tested, while the cardiac effect is duration-dependent and fails to replicate in one of two subject-split halves. We then asked whether alignment to this population-preferred respiratory phase (estimated on a held-out 99.2% of the catalog) moderates the size of the companion study’s spindle-locked geometry change (the same 190 geometry-valid events): 2 of 18 pre-specified WLS-cluster tests survived BH-FDR (d_l endpoint, event-vs-pre-spindle-baseline mode, 𝑞=0.005; d endpoint, same mode, 𝑞=0.021), and a free circular-regression sensitivity check found the implied geometry-optimal phase within 11° of the independently-estimated timing-preferred phase for d_l. We conclude that respiratory phase locks to spindle peak, not onset, in human NREM sleep - resolving a design-driven false negative in the companion study - and report a fragile secondary signal that this coupling may also weakly moderate the spindle’s own geometric consequence, framed here as an event-timing (not yet a confirmed event-efficacy) result for the sleep-EAP hypothesis, pending independent replication.Author SummaryA companion study found no evidence that sleep spindles occur at a preferred moment of the breathing or heartbeat cycle, using a small subsample of the available recordings. Re-running the same test with a corrected statistical method on the full 23,029-spindle catalog, we found that while the very start of a spindle indeed shows no preferred phase, the moment of its strongest activity does: spindles tend to peak roughly a third of the way toward full inhalation, consistently across 24 sleeping adults and several independent checks. A weaker, less consistent link to the heartbeat cycle was also found. We further found a fragile hint that how closely a spindle’s peak matches this preferred breathing phase relates to the size of that spindle’s effect on brain-state geometry, though this needs independent replication.1.IntroductionA companion three-dataset study, RVC_SLEEP (Langell, 2026), replicated a spindle-locked state-space recon­figuration (a rise in the d_n geometry endpoint) across three independent overnight-PSG datasets, then tested whether pre-event embodied-anchor (EAP) state moderates that effect. Its most mechanistically direct test (R6b, “Mål B”) asked whether spindle onset preferentially occurs at a particular cardiac or respiratory phase, using RichSleep’s phase-resolved export (event_phase_v2). That test was null at every level tested: 0/48 within-subject Rayleigh tests and 0/2 population-level second-order Rayleigh tests, on a 190-event, geometry-valid, N2-staged subsample.An independent theoretical review of that result (internal document, articles/Rythmic_Variance_Control_in_Sleep_theory/plan/02_response.md) identified two problems with treating that null as conclusive. First, the 190-event subsample discards 99% of RichSleep’s spindle catalog (23,029 raw detected events across the same 24 subjects) for reasons unrelated to the phase-locking question itself - the subsample requires N2-staging and geometry validity, neither of which the phase-locking hypothesis needs. Second, the review’s own worked critique of an earlier proposed correction (shuffling subject labels on the pooled angle set) noted that such a shuffle leaves the pooled circular-uniformity statistic invariant and therefore does not construct a valid null at all; it instead recommended a subject-specific random-phase-rotation surrogate, among other pseudo-replication-safe alternatives. It further noted that two external, non-NDT PSG studies - Ghibaudo, Juventin, Buonviso et al. (Ghibaudo et al., 2024) and Schreiner, Petzka, Staudigl, Staresina (Schreiner et al., 2023) - had already established population-level respiration-spindle phase-locking in independent human samples using a pooled, all-events design rather than the hierarchical per-subject-then-second-order design RVC_SLEEP used, raising the possibility that the RichSleep null was a design/power artifact rather than a genuine absence of coupling.Core questionDoes a corrected, pseudo-replication-safe permutation reanalysis of RichSleep’s full spindle catalog recover population-level cardiac/respiratory phase-locking that the companion study’s underpowered, two-stage design missed - and if so, at spindle onset, or at some other reference point in the event?This article reports that reanalysis. It finds that onset-phase locking remains null even at full statistical power, but that spindle peak phase - the moment of maximal amplitude within the detected event, not its start - shows a robust population-level respiratory coupling and a more fragile cardiac one. Because a timing result (does phase predict when an event occurs, or here, what phase an event’s peak lands at) is conceptually distinct from an efficacy result (does phase alignment change the consequence of an event that has already occurred), we additionally report a new, secondary analysis testing whether alignment to the population-preferred respiratory phase moderates the size of RVC_SLEEP’s own spindle-locked geometry effect on the same 190 geometry-valid events used there.2.Theory2.1.RVC/EAP tiering and the timing/efficacy distinctionWithin Noetic Diffusion Theory (NDT), sleep spindles are treated as candidate Rhythmic Variance Control (RVC) events - endogenous, TRN-implemented, rhythm-defined windows of transient gating-precision change (Langell, 2025). The Embodied Anchoring Principle (EAP) is explicitly tiered as a secondary, Tier-2 exten­sion of this core mechanism, proposing that peripheral bodily rhythms (cardiac, respiratory) can additionally condition the same latent landscape - “intended for a later wave of interoception-focused… studies,” per the theory document’s own framing, not a claim on equal evidentiary footing with the Tier-1 RVC core.The reviewed reformulation (02_response.md) sharpens EAP’s sleep-specific form into two separable, non-exclusive pathways, which this article adopts as its organizing distinction:•Event-timing pathway. Does body phase help determine when an endogenous RVC event (here, a spindle, or a specific feature of it such as its amplitude peak) occurs, i.e. is event phase non-uniformly distributed relative to the cardiac/respiratory cycle?•Event-efficacy pathway. Given that an event has occurred, does the body’s phase at that moment change how strongly the event reconfigures state-space geometry - i.e. does phase-alignment moderate the event’s own downstream consequence?R2-R5 below test the timing pathway (replacing RVC_SLEEP’s underpowered onset-referenced version with a corrected, full-catalog reanalysis); R6 tests the efficacy pathway directly, using RVC_SLEEP’s own geometry-valid subsample.2.2.Corrected permutation-null constructionThe statistic used throughout is the first-harmonic mean resultant length 𝑅1=|⟨𝑒𝑖𝜑⟩| (Rayleigh’s classical concentration measure) plus a second-harmonic term 𝑅2=|⟨𝑒2𝑖𝜑⟩|, combined into an omnibus 𝑇2=𝑅21+𝑅22 sensitive to axial/bimodal alternatives that 𝑅1 alone can miss. Rather than an asymptotic Rayleigh 𝑝-value, every 𝑝-value here is a Monte Carlo permutation 𝑝-value against a subject-specific random-phase-rotation null: each permutation draws one i.i.d. Uniform(0,2𝜋) rotation per subject and adds it to all of that subject’s phases. This destroys any common population-level preferred phase while exactly preserving each subject’s event count, within-subject clustering, and the unequal per-subject sampling that a naive pooled test would otherwise be vulnerable to - the pseudo-replication-safe alternative the theoretical review recommended in place of a subject-label shuffle. Full formulas are in S1.1.3.Methods3.1.DatasetRichSleep (RichSleep contributors, 2026) - the same 24-subject, whole-night PSG dataset used in RVC_SLEEP’s R6/R6b - is the sole dataset analyzed here. No phase-resolved export exists for RVC_SLEEP’s other two datasets (ANPHY, BOAS), so this article does not attempt a multi-dataset replication of the timing result; this is a stated limitation, not an oversight (Discussion).3.2.NMD pipeline and phase-resolved exportAnalyses use RichSleep’s event_phase_v2 export (NMD run neuralmanifolddynamics_RichSleep_20260709_100219, identical to RVC_SLEEP’s own RichSleep run; geometry is unaffected by the resp_anchor_index sign fix documented there, S1.0), which provides, per de­tected spindle, onset_sec, peak_sec (the amplitude-envelope maximum within the event), duration_sec, and cardiac/respiratory phase at both reference points (phi_cardiac_onset, phi_cardiac_peak, phi_resp_onset, phi_resp_peak). Phase conventions (documented in phase_resolved_v1/run_meta.json): 𝜑resp∈(−𝜋,𝜋), 0= full inhale, (−𝜋,0)= rising/inhale, (0,𝜋)= falling/exhale; 𝜑cardiac∈[0,2𝜋), 0= nearest R-peak, 𝜋= mid-cycle.3.3.Primary and secondary endpoint familiesPrimary family (well-powered, confirmatory): onset-phase, × 2 channels (phi_cardiac_onset, phi_resp_onset) × 2 samples (full catalog, 𝑛=23029; quality-filtered, cardiac_phase_quality≥0.7 and resp_phase_quality=1.0, 𝑛=20877) = 4 tests, BH-FDR corrected jointly (both 𝑅1- and 𝑇2-based 𝑝-values reported).Secondary/exploratory family (not FDR-corrected against the primary family): the identical machinery applied to peak-phase channels (phi_cardiac_peak, phi_resp_peak) on the same two samples, plus all four channels re-applied to the original 190-event geometry-valid subsample for direct comparability with RVC_SLEEP’s R6b numbers; a categorical cross-check (inhale_at_onset subject-level proportion vs. 0.5); and, retained for context, per-subject Rayleigh tests plus a second-order Rayleigh test on the 24 subject-level mean directions (both already pseudo-replication-safe by construction and unaffected by the permutation-null critique).3.4.Robustness protocol for the peak-phase findingFour checks, run only once a candidate peak-phase effect was identified (R3), before it is treated as anything beyond exploratory:1.Phase-convention calibration - re-verifying inhale_at_onset’s internal consistency against the docu­mented convention, and reporting preferred peak-phase angles with an explicit physiological label.2.Onset-to-peak advance / duration-tercile stratification - characterizing the onset-to-peak phase advance (peak_sec−onset_sec, converted to a phase rotation) and re-testing within three duration terciles (short/mid/long), to rule out a duration-tail-driven artifact.3.Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) jackknife - re-running both the second-order and pooled-permutation tests 24 times, once per dropped subject, to check for single-subject influence.4.Deterministic split-half replication - re-running both tests on two independent 12-subject halves (alpha­betically first vs. last), the strongest single check for whether an effect is population-general or driven by a subset of subjects.3.5.Secondary analysis: peak-phase alignment moderation of spindle-locked geometryA new analysis (this article’s only new statistical test, 13_peak_phase_alignment_moderation.py) asks whether alignment to the population-preferred respiratory peak phase moderates RVC_SLEEP’s own spindle-locked geometry effect. To avoid circularity, the population-preferred phase 𝜇resp,peak is estimated as the subject-balanced second-order circular mean of phi_resp_peak on the held-out 22,839 events (the full catalog minus the 190 geometry-valid events used below - under 1% of the catalog, costing negligible power). For each of the 190 geometry-valid events, alignment𝑒=cos(𝜑resp,peak,𝑒−𝜇resp,peak)∈[−1,1] (high when that event’s peak lands at the population-preferred phase). This is regressed against the geometry delta (endpoint−baseline, event_control or event_prefar mode, identical to RVC_SLEEP’s Mål C machinery: WLS with weights 1/𝑛events,subject, cluster-robust SE by subject, mean-centered nuisance covariates) across all 9 geometry endpoints × 2 delta modes = 18 tests, BH-FDR corrected jointly - the identical family size/design as RVC_SLEEP’s own Mål C, for direct comparability. Only the validated respiratory channel is tested (cardiac-peak is excluded a priori, having already been downgraded to fragile in this article’s own R4-R5). A secondary, exploratory circular-regression check (delta∼cos(𝜑resp,peak)+sin(𝜑resp,peak)+covariates, not folded into the 18-test family) additionally asks whether the data-implied “geometry-optimal” phase direction coincides with the independently-estimated timing-preferred phase 𝜇resp,peak.4.Results4.1.R1: Data coverageSample𝑁 subj.𝑁 eventsFull raw catalog (event_phase_v2)2423,029Quality-filtered (cardiac_phase_quality≥0.7, resp_phase_quality=1.0)2420,877Geometry-valid, N2-staged subsample (RVC_SLEEP R6b’s population)24190Table 1: Sample coverage. What was a power/design artifact, per the theoretical review’s H1, was testing the wrong reference point at insufficient power: spindle peak phase, tested on the full catalog with a corrected permutation null, shows a robust, population-level respiratory coupling (R3-R5) that four independent robustness checks confirm holds outside the specific subsample and test design that produced the original null. This is best read as resolving a design-driven false negative, not as contradicting the companion study’s own reported result on its own subsample and reference point - Table Table 2′s geometry-valid-subsample row reproduces RVC_SLEEP’s R6b numbers essentially exactly.5.2.Relation to external literatureGhibaudo et al. (Ghibaudo et al., 2024) report population-level, non-uniform spindle-occurrence phase relative to the respiratory cycle in an independent 20-subject PSG sample, using a pooled, all-events design; Schreiner et al. (Schreiner et al., 2023) report that SO-spindle-complex timing couples to respiratory phase (peaking near in­spiration) and, further, that coupling strength predicts memory-reactivation fidelity. Both operationalize spindle timing as an onset- or occurrence-referenced quantity, and both report inspiration- or peri-inspiration-favoring preferred phases - broadly consistent in direction with this article’s peak-phase preferred angle (−122.6°, i.e. the rising/inhale limb, 32% of the way to full inhale-peak), though not directly comparable in reference point (their onset/occurrence vs. this article’s envelope peak) or in the exact phase convention used. This article’s own onset-phase test remains null on the same dataset, which is itself informative: it is not that RichSleep lacks any respiratory-spindle coupling detectable by a corrected, well-powered test (as the two external studies would predict it should), but that the coupling in this dataset is specifically carried by the envelope peak rather than the onset - a genuine, reportable difference in where within the event the coupling is strongest, not a failure to replicate the general phenomenon. A separate line of work reports that respiration-brain coupling undergoes a phase shift, and becomes more consistent rather than simply weaker, moving from wakefulness into deeper sleep (“Respiratory Coordination of Excitability States across the Human Wake-Sleep Cycle,” 2025) - a state-dependent-reorganization picture broadly compatible with finding a genuine, reference-point-specific coupling in NREM sleep rather than either a simple continuation or a simple absence of a wake-state pattern.5.3.Interpreting R6 via the timing/efficacy distinctionPer the timing/efficacy framing (Theory), R3-R5 establish an event-timing result: respiratory phase predicts a feature of when (or more precisely, at what phase) a spindle’s peak occurs. R6 asks the logically separate event-efficacy question: does that phase additionally predict how strongly the spindle reconfigures geometry? The answer here is a fragile “maybe”: 2/18 tests survive family-wise correction, one sensitivity check (Wilcoxon) agrees for both, two others (t-test, sign-flip permutation) do not fully agree, and the finding emerged from testing only after the timing result was already established (a secondary analysis motivated by, not independent of, R3-R5). Per the theoretical review’s own outcome table, this pattern - “timing effect present, efficacy effect small/inconsistent” - is one of several a priori plausible readings, and is treated here as exactly that: a hint worth a dedicated, pre-registered, independently-replicated test, not a second confirmed finding riding on the first.5.4.Limitations•Post-hoc/exploratory origin. The peak-phase finding (R3) emerged from a secondary sweep run specifically because the pre-specified onset-phase test was null; while the four robustness checks in R4 were run indepen­dently of that motivation, the initial discovery was not pre-registered.•Single-dataset. RichSleep is the only one of RVC_SLEEP’s three datasets with a phase-resolved export; ANPHY and BOAS cannot be used to test cross-dataset replication of the timing result.•No endogenous-strength covariate. Neither R3-R5 nor R6 include an SO-spindle-coupling or spindle-amplitude covariate (not present in the current NMD export schema), so this article cannot yet distinguish “phase moderates a spindle’s own strength, which independently moderates geometry” from a more direct phase-geometry link, per the theoretical review’s own recommended measurement model (02_response.md S5).•Geometry-outcome resolution ceiling. RVC_SLEEP’s coords_9d geometry export is 1 value per 30 s epoch (phase_resolved_v1/run_meta.json); R6′s alignment-moderation test inherits this ceiling, so it cannot itself be phase-resolved beyond the resolution of its 190-event, epoch-level geometry outcome, independent of how precisely phase itself is measured.•R6 is exploratory and fragile. 𝑁=190 events is modest, only 2/18 tests survive FDR, and the companion sensitivity checks disagree on one of the two survivors (S3); this is stated plainly rather than downplayed.5.5.ConclusionSleep spindles’ amplitude peak - not their onset - phase-locks to the respiratory cycle in human NREM sleep, a robust, population-level, subject-consistent effect recovered by a corrected, full-catalog permutation reanalysis of a companion study’s original, underpowered null. A weaker, duration-dependent, and only partially-repli­cating cardiac counterpart is also present. A new, exploratory secondary analysis provides fragile evidence that alignment to this preferred phase may additionally moderate the size of the companion study’s own spindle-locked geometry effect, framed here as an event-timing result with a tentative, not-yet-confirmed link to event efficacy - a distinction this article’s theoretical framing treats as essential to avoid overclaiming a mechanistic role for phase beyond what the data currently support.6.AcknowledgementsThe author thanks the RichSleep dataset contributors for the underlying recordings, and acknowledges the external theoretical review (02_response.md) whose specific, falsifiable recommendations directly motivated this reanalysis.BibliographyGhibaudo, V., Juventin, M., Buonviso, N., & others.
