### What I think `sl_003` establishes

I would classify the evidence like this.

**Internal validated result:** propofol substantially relocates the trajectory in the baseline-derived spectral frame. \(d_z=+1.15\) for first sedation versus baseline is now the strongest geometrically interpretable effect in this study. Recovery remains displaced (\(d_z=+0.45\)), although the interpretation of that residual displacement is unresolved.

**Internal validated methodological result:** the matched-state C3 test is **NOT_TESTABLE**, not negative. That distinction is important. There simply is not enough shared support to evaluate the conditional-law question adequately.

**Plausible interpretation:** the previous recording-local decreases in `chart_drift_b_hat_norm` and `diffusion_total` are at least partly induced by the coordinate/reference construction. Once the coordinate frame is fixed to awake baseline, the dominant phenomenon becomes state relocation rather than reduced one-step speed/spread.

**Rejected path:** treating exported recording-local MNPS coordinates as though “the same coordinate” were necessarily the same physical/neural state across phases.

That last point actually reinforces an important boundary already built into NDT v1.0: the measurement chart is an operational interface, not the latent process, and downstream dynamical quantities inherit the validity limits of the chart used to estimate them.  NeuralManifoldDynamics makes essentially the same contract-level distinction: the exported coordinates are release-fixed proxy measurements, not uniquely identified latent neural coordinates. 

### I would now change the Apollo paper architecture

I would **not** make the 132-test L1 panel the center of the manuscript anymore.

The paper should have three evidential layers:

1. **State relocation — primary empirical result.**
   Baseline → first sedation produces a large displacement in the baseline-fixed chart. Later sedation and recovery then describe the trajectory of that displacement.

2. **Recording-local dynamical screen — secondary/discovery result.**
   In the exported local chart, first sedation shows reduced `chart_drift_b_hat_norm`, `diffusion_total`, and `diffusion_a_hat_frob`, with moderate-large effect sizes but no FDR survival. These are explicitly *coordinate-conditional exploratory observations*.

3. **C3 falsification/support test — currently unresolved.**
   The baseline-anchor analysis shows why the dynamical screen cannot presently be promoted: common support collapses, leaving only seven adequately matched subjects. Where matches exist, no meaningful speed/spread effect emerges, but precision is inadequate. Therefore **NDT-C3 is neither supported nor falsified**.

That is quite a strong scientific narrative because the analysis actually challenged its own attractive initial interpretation.

### The most important conceptual result

There is an interesting NDT-compatible dissociation here, but it is different from the one I initially proposed.

It is approximately:

$$
\text{propofol} \rightarrow
\boxed{\text{large state relocation}}
$$

while

$$
\boxed{\text{conditional future-law alteration at fixed state}}
$$

remains unknown.

That distinction is fundamental to NDT. The theory explicitly separates **“Where is the system?”** from **“What can happen from here?”** and **“What changes what can happen?”** 

Apollo currently gives you evidence mainly for the first question. That does not establish the distinctive C2/C3 claims, but it provides a particularly useful empirical boundary condition for them.

### What I would do next

I would now make **one small freeze**, rather than another exploratory analysis.

Freeze the *occupancy/state-relocation result* as the Apollo primary line using exactly the already-defined baseline-derived feature frame. Do not introduce new metrics. The confirmatory family can be very small: first sedation − baseline as the primary contrast, with later sedation and recovery as ordered secondary contrasts. If the current occupancy scalar is mathematically fixed and preregistrable, freeze that exact scalar rather than searching for an improved state-distance measure.

That would let the manuscript make a clean claim independent of the old local-chart L1 screen:

> **Propofol sedation relocates neural activity away from the individual's awake reference geometry.**

Then keep `b/a` as discovery and `sl_002`/`sl_003` as the explicit attempt to determine whether relocation was accompanied by a conditional-law change.

After that, **LOR/ROR should be the next genuinely new experimental branch**, not another metric family. It answers a different question: whether the state relocation changes specifically around behavioural responsiveness rather than simply across protocol-defined drug phases. If LOR/ROR metadata give adequate temporal support, that could turn an otherwise generic sedation paper into a much more informative NDT study.

### One subtle point I would preserve in the manuscript

The lack of matched support is not merely an inconvenience. It tells us something geometrically interesting:

**propofol may move the system far enough that the counterfactual “awake versus sedated at the same measured state” becomes rare in this representation.**

That is *not* evidence for C3. But it is a meaningful finding about the overlap structure of the state distributions. I would report the match-fraction collapse prominently, perhaps in the main figure, because it explains *why* C3 cannot currently be tested rather than making the reader wonder whether the test simply failed.
