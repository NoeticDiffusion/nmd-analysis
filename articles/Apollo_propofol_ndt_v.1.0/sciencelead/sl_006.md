This is a good freeze. `sl_005` gives Apollo a much cleaner center of gravity, and I would now treat the occupancy result as the **main empirical spine of the paper**.

The strongest point is that you have now separated three things that were previously at risk of being conflated: **state relocation**, **local one-step dynamics**, and **matched-state conditional dynamics**. NDT itself explicitly distinguishes present-state geometry from the future-law quantities built on top of it, so this hierarchy is theoretically aligned rather than merely editorial. 

My read of the evidence is:

* **Internal validated:** in a baseline-fixed eight-feature spectral frame, propofol produces a large state relocation at first sedation, a still larger relocation at later protocol sedation, and incomplete return of that scalar at recovery.
* **Internal validated methodological boundary:** common support collapses enough that the C3 matched-state test is not adequately testable.
* **Plausible interpretation:** sedation moves the measured system into a different part of state space so strongly that awake-versus-sedated counterfactuals at matched \(z\) become rare.
* **Not established:** whether the local transition law changes independently of state location; whether recovery displacement means incomplete pharmacological reversal; whether any of this tracks unconsciousness specifically.

I would also preserve the post-hoc status very prominently. The first-sedation and recovery occupancy values had already been seen before the freeze, so this is not confirmatory in the strict sense. The later-sedation contrast is cleaner prospectively relative to `sl_005`, but because it is an ordered secondary on the same promoted scalar, I would still avoid overselling it as independent confirmation.

The most important pattern now is the trajectory:

$$
\text{baseline}
\rightarrow
\text{first sedation}
\rightarrow
\text{later protocol sedation}
\rightarrow
\text{recovery}
$$

with occupancy roughly

That is a striking shape. It suggests a dose/protocol-phase ordering in this specific baseline-referenced chart, while also showing that recovery is not simply the mirror image of induction. But I would phrase that as **trajectory structure**, not as monotonic depth of unconsciousness.

The next branch should indeed be **behavior-anchored LOR/ROR**, and I would keep it separate rather than mixing it into the occupancy freeze. The exact next question should be:

That branch can test whether the state-relocation line becomes more biologically meaningful when anchored to behavior rather than protocol phase. It also gives you a cleaner way to distinguish “drug exposure” from “responsiveness transition.”
