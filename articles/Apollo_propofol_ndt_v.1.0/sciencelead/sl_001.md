I think you have the beginnings of a **much better paper than a simple “propofol changes NDT metrics” paper**. The interesting signal is not broad collapse across all levels. It is a selective **L1 drift–diffusion effect with weak Jacobian-α and currently non-estimable L2/L3**. That maps unusually well onto NDT v1.0, which explicitly says that drift \(b\), diffusion \(a\), Jacobian \(J\), and finite-horizon accessibility are distinct objects and need not move together. 

I would therefore make the next research question:

> **Does propofol alter the local chart-conditional transition law primarily through drift and stochastic spread, rather than through a generalized Jacobian/stability collapse—and do those differences persist when present state is matched?**

That is much closer to the actual load-bearing NDT claims C2/C3 than simply showing that sedation occupies a different part of MNPS. NDT-C2 asks whether future-law quantities add information beyond current state/static summaries, and C3 asks whether different physiological/control conditions can produce different futures from similar measured states. 

I would proceed in this order:

1. **Freeze the present run as discovery and stop expanding the 132-test panel.** Do not retroactively reinterpret q=0.24 as confirmatory. The current result is an *internal exploratory result*: a substantial and coherent L1 signal deserving a targeted replication. The 132-test BH correction answers “did anything survive this broad screen?”, not “is the specific drift/diffusion hypothesis false?”

2. **Define a tiny prospective primary family for G3/G4.** I would use two co-primary quantities for the next locked test: `chart_drift_b_hat_norm` and `diffusion_total`, specifically for **first sedation − baseline**. Correct those two with Holm. `diffusion_a_hat_frob` is probably better treated as a diffusion-magnitude sensitivity endpoint rather than a third primary if it largely measures the same phenomenon. Then predeclare **recovery − first sedation** as the directional reversibility test. Jacobian \(\alpha\) should be retained as a predefined dissociation endpoint rather than dropped because it was null.

3. **Resolve the reference-frame issue before the confirmatory run.** Subject anchoring is conceptually appropriate for a within-person pharmacological question, but the exact anchor matters: a subject reference that pools all phases is not identical to asking “how far has this person moved from their own awake baseline?” The NMD framework itself distinguishes subject-anchored pharmacological questions from cohort-anchored between-person questions.  I would therefore run a frozen sensitivity with a **baseline-derived within-subject reference applied unchanged to sedation and recovery**. If the \(b/a\) effect survives both the existing subject-anchored contract and the baseline-reference version, that substantially strengthens it. Cohort anchoring could be a secondary absolute-frame sensitivity, not the primary pharmacological analysis.

4. **Do not call this an unconsciousness result unless you have an actual responsiveness/LOR anchor.** Your phase-3 limitation is important. At present, the clean paper is about **graded propofol sedation and recovery**. If behavioral loss-of-responsiveness markers exist, make a separate LOR/ROR segmentation. The older ds006623 propofol analysis is useful precisely because its segments were behaviorally anchored to awake, unresponsive, and recovery; it already showed substantial first-order effects while second-order results were more qualified. 

5. **Make a matched-state C3 experiment the flagship analysis.** This, to me, is the highest-value next experiment. Within each subject, find baseline and sedation windows occupying approximately the **same current MNPS location** \(x_t\), using a frozen caliper/common-support rule. Then ask whether their subsequent increments or estimated \(b(x)\) and \(a(x)\) differ. Even better, repeat with matching on MNPS plus basic spectral covariates. If two windows are “in the same place” but first-sedation windows have systematically different future increments/spread, you have a direct empirical approximation of the NDT-C3 statement

$$
K_h^{x|\gamma_1}(x,\cdot)\neq K_h^{x|\gamma_2}(x,\cdot)
$$

at matched \(x\). That is considerably stronger evidence for NDT than a conventional baseline-versus-sedation mean difference. 

6. **Use the L2/L3 failure as an estimator boundary, not something to rescue by lowering the gate.** \(n=1–8\) says these objects are not currently supported by this sampling regime. NMD's own validity discipline is that serialized numbers do not automatically constitute qualified operators, and insufficient local support should remain unavailable rather than be extrapolated.  I would instead validate a **direct finite-horizon transition/readout** in the NDT simulator under the Apollo sampling cadence. For example, empirical \(h=1,2,\ldots\) displacement covariance or a short-horizon predictive ellipsoid can test whether the L1 diffusion contraction actually produces narrower nearby future distributions without requiring a stable history-\(\Phi\) estimator. Only promote it to the propofol paper if the simulator establishes that the estimator works in this sampling/support regime.

7. **Then test NDT-C2 as incremental predictive information.** A particularly clean analysis would compare, under leave-one-subject-out validation, a state-only model of future increments against a model that also knows sedation/control condition or the frozen L1 \(b/a\) summaries. The critical question is not “can we classify propofol?” but whether **future-state prediction improves beyond current MNPS position and standard spectral variables**. That is almost exactly the empirical burden that NDT-C2 sets for itself. 

The emerging result hierarchy would then be unusually clean. **Primary:** propofol produces reduced drift magnitude and reduced diffusion/spread during initial sedation. **Dissociation:** this is not accompanied by a comparable change in Jacobian spectral abscissa. **Reversibility:** the L1 quantities move back toward baseline in recovery. **Conditional-future test:** at matched current state, sedation still alters subsequent transition statistics. **Non-result:** higher-order L2/L3 quantities cannot currently be estimated with adequate support.

That would support a considerably more precise NDT claim than “anesthesia collapses neural geometry.” It would instead say something like:

> *Propofol changes the local law of neural-state evolution primarily at the level of drift and stochastic spread, while local modal stability remains comparatively preserved.*

That distinction is exactly what the current NDT manuscript is built to accommodate: present state, local drift, diffusion geometry, Jacobian deformation, and reachable futures are deliberately separated rather than collapsed into one “consciousness metric.”  NMD likewise describes its charts and Jacobians as versioned measurement objects rather than the latent neural state itself. 

The **single experiment I would run next** is therefore the **matched-state baseline-vs-first-sedation transition test**. Before adding more metrics, I would freeze its matching rule, primary \(b/a\) endpoints, support gates, nulls, and held-out scoring. If that works, the Apollo paper stops being merely another pharmacological separation study and becomes a fairly direct test of the NDT proposition: **same measured state, different possible futures**.
