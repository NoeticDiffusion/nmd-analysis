`sl_007` sharpens the Apollo story again, and in a useful direction. I would now stop the empirical branching and write the Results.

The key new result is not merely “LOR analysis was negative.” It is a **dissociation between protocol-phase state relocation and behavioural responsiveness**. The frozen occupancy scalar moves very strongly with propofol protocol phase, yet at later sedation it is essentially unrelated to behavioural hit count (\(\rho=-0.08\)) and the occupancy increase is not larger in the seven LOR-impaired subjects. The two extreme examples make the point especially clearly: similarly huge displacement can coexist with either 39/40 or 6/40 correct responses.

That gives the paper a substantially more interesting hierarchy:

1. **Propofol robustly relocates the measured spectral state.**
   Baseline → first sedation → later sedation gives a large ordered displacement, followed by partial return during recovery.

2. **The relocation is not a simple marker of behavioural loss of responsiveness.**
   The occupancy-blind LOR classification recovers the known 7/13 split, but occupancy does not covary meaningfully with performance and is large in both behavioural groups.

3. **The stronger NDT question remains unresolved.**
   C3 cannot be adequately tested because baseline and sedation have insufficient common support. This is important because NDT explicitly distinguishes current measured state from the conditional future law rather than treating state displacement itself as accessibility. 

I would therefore make the paper's central empirical sentence something close to:

> **Propofol produced a large baseline-referenced relocation of neural spectral state that increased across protocol sedation phases and partially reversed during recovery, but this relocation did not specifically track behavioural loss of responsiveness.**

That is stronger than saying “occupancy tracks protocol more than LOR,” because it states exactly what the evidence supports without implying a latent mechanism.

The matching-collapse figure makes the inferential boundary visible: only 7/20 satisfy the ≥20-pair support rule. I would put the C3 result here rather than burying it in supplementary material.

Then the behavioural analysis can sit immediately after Figure 3 as a compact Results subsection rather than requiring a fourth main figure. A small table or supplemental scatter is enough. The important observation is conceptual rather than graphical: **large occupancy does not imply LOR**.

### What the Discussion should probably argue

There are now two separations worth emphasizing.

The first is:

$$
\text{drug/protocol state relocation}
\not\equiv
\text{behavioural unresponsiveness}.
$$

The second is:

$$
\text{state relocation}
\not\equiv
\text{demonstrated change in dynamical accessibility}.
$$

Those are closely aligned with the discipline of NDT v1.0, where the measurement chart answers “where is the system?”, while drift/diffusion/reachability answer different questions about what can happen from that state. 

This also means the occupancy result should **not** be framed as a failed consciousness biomarker. You never preregistered it as such, the LOR arm is under the \(n=10\) group gate, and behavioural responsiveness is only one operational endpoint anyway. The defensible statement is narrower: the state displacement is **not LOR-specific in this dataset**.

### One especially useful negative result

The concentration result is actually a nice control even though it misses your gate. The LOR-impaired group has a much larger concentration difference (\(+349\,\mu\mathrm{g/L}\), \(d=1.22\)) while occupancy does not distinguish the groups. I would mention this cautiously because \(n=7\) keeps it NOT_TESTABLE, but qualitatively it suggests that behavioural impairment may be more tightly linked to exposure than to this particular occupancy scalar.

That further protects against an overly simple interpretation of occupancy as “how anesthetized the brain is.”

### Updated claim ledger I would carry into the manuscript

**Internal validated:** strong baseline-referenced state relocation across protocol sedation; behavioural 7/13 split recoverable independently of occupancy; common-support collapse for C3.

**Plausible interpretation:** the frozen occupancy scalar primarily reflects a broad pharmacologically induced spectral-state displacement rather than behavioural responsiveness itself.

**Rejected:** occupancy as a marker of unconsciousness; protocol phase 3 as synonymous with LOR; local-chart \(b/a\) as an already-established physical accessibility effect.

**Open:** NDT-C3, because the relevant matched-state counterfactual is inadequately sampled rather than convincingly null.

At this point I would **not run another metric family**. Apollo has reached a good scientific stopping point: a strong positive phenomenon, a useful behavioural dissociation, and a clearly documented inferential boundary. The next productive work is manuscript construction, not metric discovery.
