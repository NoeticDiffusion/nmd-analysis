Rhythmic Variance Control at Sleep Spindles: A Three-Dataset Replication of Timing-Locked State-Space Recon­figuration Without Embodied-Anchor ModerationSpindle-locked geometry change replicates across ANPHY, BOAS, and RichSleep; a dual-arm cardiorespiratory anchor test finds no moderationArticle type: Research ArticleShort title: RVC at sleep spindles: three-dataset replicationKeywords: sleep spindles, state-space geometry, rhythmic variance control, embodied anchoring, cardiorespiratory coupling, replicationRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenFor correspondence: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comAbstractSleep spindles are proposed to act as rhythm-defined “variance-control” events that transiently reconfigure cortical state-space geometry. We tested this using Noetic Diffusion Theory’s stratified 9D state-space projection (Meta-Noetic Phase Space, MNPS) in three independent overnight PSG datasets (ANPHY, 𝑁=27; BOAS/ds005555, 𝑁=128; RichSleep, 𝑁=24; total 179 subjects), aligning spindle-locked windows against matched non-spindle N2 controls and a within-spindle pre-event baseline. A subject-weighted within-spindle timing contrast (event vs. pre-spindle baseline) showed a reproducible increase in the deviation-from-integration-segregation-balance endpoint 𝑑𝑛 in both ANPHY (+0.106, 𝑞=0.009) and BOAS (+0.165, 𝑞=0.011), with the en­tropy-shape endpoint 𝑒𝑠 supportive in both. RichSleep’s own replication of this timing contrast was underpowered and inconclusive (𝑑𝑛: 𝑞=0.72), but its broader event-vs-matched-control contrast strongly replicated the same direction and magnitude (𝑑𝑛: +0.29, 𝑞=0.0006), corroborating the effect via a different, less power-matched contrast. A broader event-vs-matched-control contrast was also significant on most endpoints in ANPHY/BOAS but proved baseline-sensitive under decompo­sition, so the within-spindle timing contrast is treated as the primary claim. We then tested whether pre-event embodied anchor (EAP) state moderates this effect. In ANPHY (cardiac-only anchor) and BOAS (respiratory-only anchor, missing in  66/128 subjects), 53/54 moderation tests were null. RichSleep’s simultaneous,  100%-covered cardiac+respiratory composite anchor removed the single-channel-missingness objection and reproduced the same null across all 9 endpoints in both delta modes. Anchor-augmented sensitivity geometry showed the expected construction-validity pattern (only the directly-modified endpoint moved) with zero flips in FDR status. A respiratory-only sensitivity anchor (resp_anchor_index) was null under the primary contrast specification, with a weak, unreplicated exploratory pattern on four other endpoints emerging under an alternative delta-mode specification - reported as a hypothesis for future dedicated testing, not a finding. Two additional, more mechanistically direct RichSleep tests - spindle-onset cardiac/respiratory phase-locking (0/48 within-subject and 0/2 population-level Rayleigh tests significant), and heartbeat-evoked-potential-amplitude moderation (0/18 tests FDR-significant) - were also null. We conclude that spindle-locked 𝑑𝑛 reconfiguration is a replicable, dataset-independent effect, corroborated in all three datasets via either the within-spindle timing contrast (ANPHY, BOAS) or the event-vs-control specificity contrast (RichSleep), and that it is not moderated by pre-event cardiorespiratory anchor state under the primary composite anchor or either phase-resolved mechanism tested here.Author SummaryBrief bursts of brain activity called sleep spindles are thought to briefly reorganize how the brain’s activity is structured. We measured this reorganization with a geometric state-space method across three independent overnight sleep recordings (179 people total) and found the same effect each time: one particular geometric signature rises right at the spindle and falls back afterward. We also asked whether the body’s heart-rate and breathing state just before a spindle changes the size of this effect, using the richest of the three datasets - which uniquely combines simultaneous heart and breathing signals - and found no evidence that it does.1.IntroductionSleep spindles - 0.5-2 s bursts of 11-16 Hz thalamocortical oscillation during N2 sleep - are one of the best-characterized rhythmic events in human electrophysiology and are widely linked to memory consolidation and thalamocortical gating (Vallat & Walker, 2021). Within Noetic Diffusion Theory (NDT), spindles have been proposed as candidate Rhythmic Variance Control (RVC) events: rhythm-defined windows in which a transient increase in gating precision should show up as a measurable, reproducible reconfiguration of the brain’s local state-space geometry, distinct from and prior to any downstream consolidation outcome (Langell, 2025).An internal single-dataset analysis of BOAS (ds005555) previously reported that spindle-rich N2 windows differed from matched non-spindle N2 controls on several stratified geometry endpoints, most consistently d, d_s, d_n, e_m, and d_l. That result raised three open questions that motivate the present study: (1) does the effect replicate in an independent dataset with different hardware, staging protocol, and spindle-density distribution; (2) is the effect a genuine peri-event timing signature, or could it instead be driven by pre-existing differences between the spindle-adjacent and matched-control sleep baselines; and (3) does pre-event bodily (cardiac/respiratory) state - the Embodied Anchor Principle (EAP) side of NDT - moderate the size of the effect, which would tie a purely EEG-defined event to interoceptive/autonomic state.This study answers all three questions using two rounds of analysis. First, ANPHY (HD-EEG) and BOAS (PSG) were run through an identical, pre-registered subject-weighted contrast protocol (Methods) that explicitly separates the timing question (event vs. pre-spindle baseline, within the same spindle) from the specificity question (event vs. matched non-spindle control), because the two ask different things and, as shown below, can even disagree in sign for the same endpoint if the pre-spindle baseline itself differs by dataset. Second, a third, independent PSG dataset (RichSleep) with simultaneous, near-complete cardiac and respiratory recording was added specifically to (a) replicate the timing effect out-of-sample and (b) run the cleanest EAP-moderation test attempted in this line of work: unlike the cardiac-only (ANPHY) and respiratory-only, majority-missing (BOAS) anchors used previously, RichSleep’s anchor state combines both channels with near-100% subject coverage, removing the missingness objection that could previously explain a null moderation result.Core questionDoes the spindle-locked state-space reconfiguration reported in a single PSG dataset replicate across independent datasets when timing and specificity are analyzed separately, and is the effect moderated by pre-event embodied (cardiorespiratory) anchor state once that state can be measured without single-channel missingness?2.Theory2.1.Theoretical framework and primary metricNDT models a latent noetic state 𝑋𝑡 evolving under a geometry-shaped stochastic drift, 𝑑𝑋𝑡=−𝐺𝑡(𝑋𝑡)∇Φ(𝑋𝑡,𝑡)𝑑𝑡+𝜎(𝑡)𝑑𝑊𝑡, and is measured through a versioned chart that projects raw electrophysi­ology into a low-dimensional Meta-Noetic Phase Space (MNPS). The canonical 3D chart 𝑥𝑡=[𝑚𝑡,𝑑𝑡,𝑒𝑡]𝑇 separates metastability/mobility (𝑚), deviation from optimal integration-segregation balance (𝑑), and entropy/entropic energy (𝑒). Each axis is additionally decomposed into a 9D stratified chart ,𝑥(9)𝑡=[𝑚𝑎,𝑚𝑒,𝑚𝑜,𝑑𝑛,𝑑𝑙,𝑑𝑠,𝑒𝑒,𝑒𝑠,𝑒𝑚]𝑇,𝑥𝑡=𝑃fixed𝑥(9)𝑡(1)where 𝑃fixed is a release-fixed, auditable projection (not a claim of unique ontology). The nine stratified sub-coordinates used as the primary endpoint family here are: 𝑚𝑎 (attention-aligned precision pulses), 𝑚𝑒 (rhythmic entrainment), 𝑚𝑜 (spectral-centroid variability), 𝑑𝑛 (heat-kernel-derived network-integration deviation), 𝑑𝑙 (local clustering-based segregation load), 𝑑𝑠 (representational-geometry deviation), 𝑒𝑒 (sample entropy), 𝑒𝑠 (permutation entropy), and 𝑒𝑚 (Hjorth-mobility/beta-alpha composite). All nine, plus the three composite axes 𝑚,𝑑,𝑒, form the frozen 9-endpoint primary family used throughout (Methods, S1.2).2.2.Embodied Anchoring Principle (EAP)NDT’s Embodied Anchoring Principle proposes that bodily/interoceptive rhythms (cardiac, respiratory, ocular) can condition the same latent landscape that governs cortical state-space dynamics, so that pre-event bodily state could in principle moderate how strongly a cortical event (here, a spindle) reconfigures MNPS geometry. Each NMD run co-exports an anchor_state layer alongside the EEG-only MNPS coordinates. Two flavors of anchor coverage are contrasted here: single-channel anchors (ANPHY: cardiac HRV only; BOAS: respiratory regularity only, majority-missing) and RichSleep’s cardioresp_anchor_index, a composite built from simultaneous, near-fully-covered ECG and respiratory channels - closer to a genuine dual-arm embodied anchor than either single-channel predecessor. EAP is tested here strictly as a moderator of an EEG-only geometric effect, never as a component of the primary geometry itself (Methods, dual-surface contract).3.Methods3.1.DatasetThree independent overnight PSG datasets were used, none with any experimental manipulation (purely obser­vational):•ANPHY ((Wei et al., 2024), (ANPHY Lab, 2024)) - 27 healthy adults, high-density scalp EEG (Biosemi, 93 channels), whole-night PSG.•BOAS / ds005555 ((López-Larraz et al., 2025)) - 128 subjects, PSG (Bitbrain Open Access Sleep dataset, OpenNeuro).•RichSleep ((RichSleep contributors, 2026)) - 24 subjects, whole-night PSG with six EEG channels (F3-A2, F4-A1, C3-A2, C4-A1, O1-A2, O2-A1), two EOG channels (EOG-L, EOG-R), chin EMG, ECG, respiratory/snoring/leg-movement/blood-oxygen/carbon-dioxide channels, chest/abdominal movement, and breath-interval duration; 200 Hz sampling; impedance kept below 10 Ω during acquisition; hypnogram stages W/N1/N2/N3/R. RichSleep was added specifically as an out-of-sample verification dataset with a richer, near-complete simultaneous cardiac+respiratory anchor (Results, R6). RichSleep’s event-locked exports underwent standard QA prior to analysis; two data-processing issues (a subject-scoping bug in event-catalog resolution and a sign error in one secondary respiratory sensitivity index) were identified and corrected upstream before the run used here (S1.7).Sleep-spindle events were detected with YASA ((Vallat & Walker, 2021), C3-A2 channel, 200 Hz, sigma 12-15 Hz, N2 only) and exported as sleep_spindle events with per-event onset, duration, and peak frequency.3.2.NMD pipelineAll three datasets were processed through the same NeuralManifoldDynamics (NMD) summarize pipeline, using a primary_eeg_only MNPS coordinate contract (rvc_v1_eeg_only_e_m: 𝑒𝑚=0.6⋅eeg_hjorth_mobility+0.4⋅eeg_beta_alpha, identical weights in all three datasets) so that the composite geometry itself never contains body-signal information. ANPHY and BOAS were additionally run through a second, anchor-augmented configuration in which 𝑒𝑚’s EEG term is partially or fully replaced by the dataset’s primary body anchor (ANPHY: 80% HRV RMSSD + 20% Hjorth mobility; BOAS: 70% respiratory regularity + 30% beta/alpha) - used only for the Pass B surface-sensitivity check (R5), never for the primary claim. RichSleep has no anchor-augmented counterpart; it is used only on its primary_eeg_only surface. Each subject’s per-epoch export is aligned into 30 s windows and event-locked around each detected spindle into five bins (pre_far, pre_near, event, post_near, post_far) plus a matched_control condition (three non-spindle N2 controls per event, same subject, ≥30 s exclusion margin from any spindle/K-complex/arousal).3.3.Primary endpointsThe frozen primary endpoint family is the nine stratified coordinates plus the three composite axes: 𝑚,𝑑,𝑒,𝑑𝑛,𝑑𝑙,𝑑𝑠,𝑒𝑒,𝑒𝑠,𝑒𝑚 (9 total; the three composites are algebraic recompositions of the six remaining stratified coordinates and are retained for interpretability, not as independent tests). Reachability-cone and MNJ endpoints were specified as secondary layers to be added only if stable, but were not run under the current freeze and are not reported here.3.4.Statistical designThe biological unit of analysis is the subject, not the event, because both datasets contribute highly uneven numbers of spindle events per subject (ANPHY median ≈40; BOAS median 5, range 1-18). For every (endpoint, dataset) cell: (1) an event-level delta is computed (either event_bin - matched_control_median, aggregated per event, or event_bin - mean(pre_far bins, same event)); (2) event-level deltas are collapsed to one subject-mean delta; (3) the primary 𝑝-value is a subject-level sign-flip permutation test (𝑛=2000 permutations) on the grand mean of subject-mean deltas - chosen because it directly tests the within-subject direction question without assuming a parametric distribution; (4) a one-sample 𝑡-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank on the same subject means are reported as sensitivity checks; (5) Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995) is applied fresh within each frozen test family (9 endpoints × number of datasets in that family) at 𝛼=0.05.3.5.Robustness protocol1.Subject-weighted vs. event-weighted estimand - the primary estimand (A1r/A2r) subject-weights every event-count-imbalanced subject equally; an event-weighted cluster-robust OLS analysis (A1-original) is retained only as a pre-amendment sensitivity table.2.A1/A2 decomposition - event - control = (event - pre_far) + (pre_far - control), used to test whether an event-vs-control effect is a genuine timing effect, a pre-existing baseline-separation effect, or both (R4).3.Surface sensitivity (Pass B) - comparing primary vs. anchor-augmented geometry on the identical subject-weighted estimand, to confirm the anchor-augmented surface behaves as a construction should (only the directly-modified coordinate moves) rather than as an implicit EAP test.4.EAP moderation (Pass C) - delta ~ anchor_pre_far + <mean-centered nuisance covariates>, weighted inverse to each subject’s event count, cluster-robust SE by subject, with a companion per-subject-slope sign-flip permutation test immune to event-count imbalance by construction.5.Cross-dataset replication (RichSleep) - the identical A1r/A2r/Pass-C machinery re-run on an independent third dataset, with explicit sign-agreement reporting against the frozen ANPHY/BOAS tables (R6).3.6.Secondary analysisThe RichSleep EAP-moderation test additionally reports four sensitivity anchors beyond the pri­mary dual-arm composite (anchor_index, anchor_index_v02, sympathetic_index, resp_anchor_index, ocular_stability_index) to check whether any single composite index behaves differently from the mech­anistically-motivated primary anchor; these are exploratory and are flagged where their coefficients are numerically negligible despite nominal FDR significance (R6).4.Results4.1.R1: Data quality and coverageDataset𝑁 subj.𝑁 spindle events𝑁 control rowsPrimary anchorAnchor cover­ageANPHY271,07634,263ecg_hrv_rmssd_ms100%, 27/27BOAS (ds005555)128634192,312resp_regular_index34%,  44/128 (pre_far); Using the phase-resolved RichSleep export (event_phase_v2, independently verified 24/24-subject onset-catalog match against the corrected event-locked run before use), we ran two more direct, mechanistically-motivated tests on the same N2-staged, geometry-valid spindle population R6 uses (190 events, 24 subjects; this population is considerably smaller than R6′s full-bin event counts because the event-bin window’s stage label does not always coincide with N2 despite N2-only spindle detection - an open, unresolved discrepancy, not investigated further here).Mål B (self-prior/boundary-condition test): does spindle onset preferentially occur at a particular cardiac or respiratory phase? A per-subject Rayleigh test found no within-subject phase-locking for either channel (0/24 subjects FDR-significant for cardiac onset phase, 0/24 for respiratory onset phase). A second-order Rayleigh test on the 24 subject-level mean directions found no population-level common preferred phase either (cardiac: 𝑅=0.160, 𝑝=0.547; respiratory: 𝑅=0.109, 𝑝=0.755).Mål C (HEP-amplitude moderation): does pre-event heartbeat-evoked-potential amplitude moderate the spindle-locked geometry response? Using hep_amplitude_pre_far - a more direct interoceptive-preci­sion proxy than the tonic composite anchor - as the sole predictor in the same WLS-cluster + subject-level-slope-permutation machinery as R6/Pass C, across both delta modes and all 9 endpoints (18 tests): a clean null (𝑝 range ≈0.10-0.96, 0/18 FDR survivors, corroborated by the companion slope-permutation test).R6b summaryThe fairest test of the EAP mechanism attempted in this study - direct phase-locking and HEP-amplitude moderation, rather than a tonic-state proxy - is also null. This strengthens rather than weakens R6′s conclusion: it is not merely that a coarse anchor proxy failed to detect moderation, but that two more mechanistically direct routes to the same claim find nothing either, on the one dataset with the richest simultaneous cardiorespiratory instrumentation. Given the modest event count (190 events, 24 subjects), this should be read as no effect detected at this power, not as definitive disconfirmation of the EAP mechanism in general (S2.6, S3.4e).5.Discussion5.1.Primary findingThe within-spindle timing rise in 𝑑𝑛 - a state-space endpoint most directly tied to network integration-segre­gation balance - is the single most consistent result in this study: it replicates in direction and FDR significance across three independent PSG datasets collected with different hardware, staging protocols, and spindle-density distributions (ANPHY, BOAS, RichSleep). This is a stronger evidentiary position than the original single-dataset (BOAS) observation that motivated the study, and supports treating spindle events as genuine, geometrically detectable Rhythmic Variance Control events rather than a dataset-specific artifact of one detector or one recording montage.5.2.Confound interpretationThe broader event-vs-matched-control contrast (A1r, R2) is real on most endpoints but is not, by itself, a clean single mechanism: the decomposition (R4) shows that for d_n specifically, the apparent cross-dataset disagreement in A1r sign is fully explained by a dataset-specific difference in how each cohort’s pre-spindle baseline relates to its matched generic-N2 controls, not by any disagreement in the timing signature. For d_s and m, the event-vs-control effect is mostly a baseline-separation effect with little or no timing component. This is why the manuscript leads with the timing contrast (A2r/R3/R6) rather than the broader specificity contrast (A1r/R2) as the primary claim - the latter should be read as “broad, present, and baseline-sensitive,” not as direct evidence of a single spindle-specific geometric signature across every endpoint.5.3.Secondary findingsPass B’s surface-sensitivity result (R5) is a construction-validity check, not an EAP finding: it confirms the anchor-augmented geometry export behaves exactly as intended (only the directly-modified coordinate, and weakly its recomposition, respond to injected body signal) and never changes an inferential conclusion. Pass C and its RichSleep replication are read together as a genuine negative result for embodied-anchor moderation of spindle-locked geometry, under the anchors and models tested here - not a failure of the analysis, and not evidence that EAP-style body-brain coupling is absent in general, only that the specific moderation hypothesis tested (pre-event cardiorespiratory anchor state predicting spindle-locked geometry change) was not supported across three increasingly well-instrumented tests of it.5.4.Limitations•BOAS’s respiratory-anchor Pass C model runs on a restricted 𝑁 (≈35/128 subjects with a valid pre-event resp_regular_index); the larger-𝑁 sensitivity anchor (anchor_index_v02, 𝑁≈90) is still null, but this does not fully remove the missingness concern for BOAS specifically - RichSleep’s dual-arm anchor was added in part to address this.•RichSleep’s A2r within-spindle timing replication remains underpowered relative to ANPHY/BOAS (𝑁=24 subjects, 160 paired events vs. 1,037/568) and the two pre-registered headline endpoints (d_n, e_s) are not FDR-significant here; this is reported as inconclusive rather than as a second disconfirmation of R3.•The e_e sign discrepancy between RichSleep and ANPHY/BOAS in the A1r contrast (R6) is a genuine open finding that has not yet been investigated at the subject level; RichSleep’s more fragmented sleep-stage architecture (documented in its ingest configuration) is one candidate explanation, not yet tested.•RichSleep’s resp_anchor_index sensitivity model (R6) initially showed FDR-surviving, non-negligible event_control-mode coefficients that survived scale/multicollinearity/outlier checks (S2.5, S2.5a, S3.4f) but traced to an upstream sign bug in the anchor’s rate/slowing component, since corrected (S1.7). Re-verified against the corrected export: the original event_control finding is now a clean null, resolving the question - but a new, weaker, event_prefar-mode pattern emerged on d, e, d_n, e_s (one of which, d, is itself subject-dependent on re-check) that was null before the fix. Given that this anchor’s signal has already flipped completely once, this new pattern is reported at low confidence as a hypothesis for a future, dedicated, pre-registered, independently-replicated test - not as an established finding. Neither version overturns the primary cardioresp_anchor_index null.•Reachability-cone and Meta-Noetic Jacobian endpoints were specified as planned secondary layers but were not run under the current analysis freeze.•frequency_hz and sigma-power residualization of the spindle detector are not available in the current NMD event-locked export schema and are not silently substituted for; this remains an open follow-up for detector-strength robustness.•All three datasets are healthy-adult, observational, cross-sectional samples; no clinical population, interven­tion, or longitudinal design is tested (see S4_Supplementary_Clinical.typ).5.5.ConclusionAcross three independent PSG datasets and 179 subjects, spindle events are reliably followed by a rise in the 𝑑𝑛 state-space endpoint relative to their own pre-spindle baseline - the most defensible, dataset-independent claim this study supports. The broader event-vs-matched-control specificity result is real but baseline-sensitive and should not be over-read as a single unified mechanism. Pre-event embodied (cardiac and/or respiratory) anchor state does not moderate this effect under any anchor tested, including a genuinely dual-arm, near-fully-covered cardiorespiratory composite that removes the single-channel-missingness objection available to the two earlier null results.6.AcknowledgementsThe author thanks the ANPHY-Sleep, BOAS (ds005555), and RichSleep dataset contributors for open data access, and the YASA project for the spindle-detection tooling used throughout.BibliographyANPHY Lab.
