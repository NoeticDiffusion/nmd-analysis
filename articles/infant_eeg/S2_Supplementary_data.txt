A prior, pre-masking export produced somewhat different numbers for several endpoints; the pre-masking values are noted inline where the change is informative (e.g. S2.5, S2.7) but are not reported in full here.S2.1 Cohort-coordinate GEE family (primary result)Results are stored in results/figshare_infant_cohort_20260805_132612/03_cohort_coordinate_age/cohort_coordinate_gee_results.csv. All 12 cohort-coordinate spline models were FDR-supported across 70 visits/22 children. The 3D coordinate results were mobility: 𝜒2=11.28, FDR p=0.011; deviation: 𝜒2=54.72, FDR p=4.72×10−11; and entropy-related coordinate: 𝜒2=15.09, FDR p=2.09×10−3. The strongest 9D result was 𝑚𝑜 (𝜒2=155.37, FDR p=2.19×10−32); the weakest overall was 9D 𝑑𝑠 (𝜒2=7.89, raw/FDR p=0.048). They describe coordinate position in this frozen common chart and are the primary finding of this study (manuscript R2). This asymptotic GEE result should be read together with S2.2 (within/between decomposition) and S2.3 (cluster-permutation check), which qualify how robust each endpoint is.S2.2 Within/between-child age decomposition (M3)Results are stored in cohort_coordinate_within_between_decomposition.csv. At every one of the 12 end­points, the within-child spline term (3 df) is significant: raw p ranges from 7.94×10−73 (9D 𝑚𝑜, the strongest) to 0.0105 (9D 𝑑𝑠, the weakest). No endpoint’s between-child linear term (1 df) is significant at 𝛼=0.05 after FDR correction; the smallest between-term raw p is 9D 𝑒𝑚 at 0.041 (12-endpoint FDR ≈ 0.21), and most are far from significance (raw p up to 0.98 for 3D entropy-related coordinate). This directly addresses the between-child confound raised in peer_review/pr_01.md M3: the primary cohort-coordinate result is attributable to within-child movement, not to older and younger children simply differing cross-sectionally.S2.3 Child-cluster row-permutation check (M4)Results are stored in cohort_coordinate_gee_cluster_permutation.csv (500 permutations per endpoint, seed 42). Raw permutation p-values, smallest to largest: 9D 𝑚𝑜 0.002, 3D deviation 0.010, 9D 𝑑𝑛 0.016, 9D 𝑒𝑚 0.018, 9D 𝑚𝑒/𝑒𝑒 0.020 each, 9D 𝑑𝑙 0.036, 9D 𝑚𝑎 0.052, 9D 𝑒𝑠 0.056, 3D entropy-related coordinate 0.130, 3D mobility 0.140, 9D 𝑑𝑠 0.222. After BH-FDR correction of these 12 permutation p-values, 6 of 12 endpoints remain supported: 9D 𝑚𝑜 (FDR p=0.024); 3D deviation, 9D 𝑚𝑒, 9D 𝑑𝑛, 9D 𝑒𝑒, and 9D 𝑒𝑚 (FDR p=0.040 each). Six endpoints do not survive: 9D 𝑑𝑙 (FDR p=0.062), 9D 𝑚𝑎/𝑒𝑠 (FDR p=0.075 each), 3D entropy-related coordinate and 3D mobility (FDR p=0.152 each), and 9D 𝑑𝑠 (FDR p=0.222). This is a materially more conservative picture than the asymptotic GEE (S2.1) and should be treated as the more trustworthy robustness statement given only 22 clusters (peer_review/pr_01.md M4).S2.4 Cohort-coordinate direct-age metadata sensitivityThe spreadsheet-direct cohort-coordinate GEE subset included 40 visits from 22 children. Eleven of twelve endpoints remained FDR-supported; the 3D entropy-related coordinate was the only exception (raw/FDR p=0.070). In 3D, mobility and deviation had FDR p=6.62×10−4 and 3.10×10−9, respectively. All visits had resolved 9D MNJs.S2.7 Within-child direct-age metadata sensitivityAs a robustness check of S2.5, the primary 3D within-child family was re-run on the 36 visits from 18 children with spreadsheet-direct ages (direct_age_metadata_sensitivity.csv). No endpoint crossed nominal signif­icance: the smallest raw p was rotation_norm_median at 0.208 (FDR sensitivity p=0.636). This is a materially different result from the pre-masking export, where the same two endpoints (frobenius_norm_median, raw p=0.050; rotation_norm_median, raw p=0.043) crossed nominal significance before FDR correction; both are now well above 0.2 (frobenius_norm_median raw p=0.277). Because artifact-flagged segments were specifi­cally masked between the two exports, this pattern is consistent with the earlier nominal hits having been driven by residual motion artifact rather than a developmental effect, though this inference is descriptive (a before/after comparison across two non-independent exports of the same recordings), not a formal statistical test. It is reported here in full for completeness alongside the cohort-coordinate direct-age sensitivity (S2.4).S2.8 Cohort-pooled reachability family (robustness check)Results are stored in results/figshare_infant_cohort_20260805_132612/05_cohort_pooled_reachability/. All 70 visits were testable (cohort_pooled_reachability_per_visit.csv), drawing from a pooled set of 5,195 cross-session epoch-to-epoch transitions (down from 6,851 pre-masking) with a median of 8 distinct contributing children per visit after leave-one-child-out exclusion. None of the five endpoints was FDR-supported, or even nominally significant, in the primary within-child-style test (cohort_pooled_reachability_age_associations.csv): anisotropy (standardized slope 0.27, raw p=0.478, the smallest raw p in this family), log-volume (raw p=0.411), directional entropy (raw p=0.277), effective dimension (raw p=0.351), and rotational proxy (raw p=0.196). This is a materially cleaner null than the pre-masking export, whose smallest raw p (anisotropy, 0.035) was nominally significant though not FDR-supported. The direct-age-only subset (36 visits/18 children, cohort_pooled_reachability_direct_age_sensitivity.csv) showed one nominal (uncorrected) hit – rota­tional proxy (standardized slope −0.47, raw p=0.040, FDR sensitivity p=0.200) – not FDR-supported. The per-visit neighbor 𝑘-th-distance was stable across age terciles (medians 0.352, 0.292, 0.329 in the youngest, middle, and oldest thirds), so these nulls are not attributable to a strong age-density confound in the pooled neighbor search.
