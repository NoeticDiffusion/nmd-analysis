A useful state-space analysis should therefore do more than ask whether dementia differs from health in one scalar direction. It should also ask where the signal concentrates: in coarse subject summaries, in higher-dimensional decomposition, or in local dynamical coupling.This compact report applies a fixed NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 workflow to the resting-state EEG dataset ds004504, which contains Healthy, AD, and FTD groups together with Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores. The aim is intentionally narrow. We ask two bounded questions:1.Where does the clearest Healthy-versus-dementia separation appear on the currently available export surface?2.Does within-diagnosis MMSE severity align more with AD, with FTD, or with neither?This is not presented as a general dementia biomarker paper. The strongest severity-linked bin effects again localize to FTD-specific 9D and block-MNJ summaries.DiscussionThe present dataset does not support a broad claim that one single scalar neural summary separates all dementias from health or tracks severity equally across diagnoses. A tighter reading fits the data better.First, Healthy-versus-dementia separation is visible, but on the currently exported surface it appears more clearly in block-resolved Jacobian structure than in the selected scalar global MNJ metrics. Second, the within-cohort severity signal is not shared evenly by AD and FTD. The strongest continuous and binned MMSE structure is concentrated in FTD_only, where both 9D MNJ and 9AD and FTD Neural Geometry10block-MNJ repeatedly carry the signal. Third, the AD subgroup is not null, but in this compact analysis its severity structure is weaker and mostly trend-level.That pattern can be read in a restrained way. The current export suggests that dementia-related organization in this cohort may be better captured as higher-dimensional local dynamical reconfiguration than as a simple global contraction index. It does not yet justify a claim that FTD is “more geometric” than AD in any general sense. It only shows that, in this dataset and export surface, FTD contributed the clearest MMSE-linked dynamic organization.LimitationsThis manuscript has several important limits.1.It is a single-dataset report with modest subgroup sizes, especially once MMSE tertiles are formed.2.The article is constrained by the currently exported paper-facing surface. The present draft cannot test broader first-order MNPS or reachability hypotheses because those layers were not the stable article-facing outputs in this pass.3.Many direct group contrasts were only uncorrected-significant or trend-level after BH-FDR.4.MMSE is clinically useful but coarse, and these analyses are cross-sectional rather than longi­tudinal.5.The strongest FTD bin effects come from small cells and therefore demand replication before being treated as robust subtype markers.ConclusionThe current ds004504 analysis supports a bounded conclusion: resting-state dementia-related separation is detectable in a fixed NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 frame, but in this export 10AD and FTD Neural Geometry11it localizes more clearly to higher-dimensional and block-level local dynamics than to the selected scalar global Jacobian summaries. Within that surface, the strongest severity-linked organization is concentrated in FTD_only, while AD_only remains weaker and mostly trend-level.
