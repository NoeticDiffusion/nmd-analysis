Article type: Research Article / Compact Empirical ReportResting-State Neural Geometry Across Depressive Symptom BurdenA compact NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 report for ds003478Short title: Depressive Symptom GeometryRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenCorresponding author: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comKeywords: depression, BDI, EEG, NeuralManifoldDynamics, reachability cones, MNJAbstractWe analyzed the resting-state openneuro EEG dataset ds003478 with a fixed NeuralManifoldDy­namics 2.1 workflow, using Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) score as the primary clinical axis. The article-facing export included global 3D and 9D MNPS summaries, global 3D and 9D MNJ summaries, 9 x 9 block-MNJ, reachability cones, tier2_emmi, and distribution summaries. In the predefined low-versus-high symptom contrast (BDI 0-13 vs BDI 20-63), the strongest selected ef­fects did not appear in 3D summaries. They concentrated in reachability (tube_log_eig_var_mean, d = −0.678, q = 0.00296; closely related tube_log_det_mean, d = 0.735, q = 0.00296), 9D MNJ (rotational_power_mean, d = 0.569, q = 0.00317), and a distribution summary (e_m_mad_sigma, d = 0.688, q = 0.0132). Continuous BDI analyses showed the same ordering across all valid participants: reachability (rho = −0.343, q = 0.00350), 9D MNJ (rho = 0.310, q = 0.00364), and distribution summary (rho = 0.358, q = 0.00226) outperformed 3D summaries. Clinical BDI bands 1Depressive Symptom Geometry2sharpened the same pattern. BDI 0-13 vs 14-19 showed especially strong reachability (d = −0.831, q = 7.46e-6) and distribution (d = 1.727, q = 5.01e-4) effects, while BDI 14-19 vs 20-63 was comparatively weak. The bounded conclusion is that, in this dataset and export surface, depressive symptom burden is more visible in higher-dimensional local dynamics and reachability geometry than in coarse 3D summaries, and the dominant organization is a low-to-elevated symptom regime separation rather than a clean monotonic gradient within the high-BDI range.IntroductionDepressive symptom burden need not manifest as a single scalar reduction in resting-state neural dynamics. If symptom load changes how local trajectories spread, rotate, or retain access to nearby state-space, the clearest signal may appear in higher-dimensional local summaries rather than in the coarsest global descriptors.This compact report applies a fixed NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 workflow to the resting-state EEG dataset ds003478 (OpenNeuro DOI: 10.18112/openneuro.ds003478.v1.1.0), using Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) score as the primary clinical axis. The aim is intentionally narrow. We ask three bounded questions:1.Where does the clearest low-versus-high BDI separation appear on the current export surface?2.Do continuous BDI associations reproduce the same ordering of signal across blocks?3.Do clinically interpretable BDI bands (0-13, 14-19, 20-63) suggest a smooth severity gradient or a stronger regime contrast?This is not presented as a diagnostic major depression biomarker paper. A tighter reading fits the data better.First, the strongest signal in the current export surface lies in reachability and 9D local dynamics, not in the selected 3D MNPS or 3D MNJ summaries. Second, continuous BDI associa­8Depressive Symptom Geometry9tions and banded contrasts point in the same direction, which increases confidence that the signal is not an artifact of one single thresholding choice. Third, the structure looks more like a low-to-elevated symptom regime separation than a smooth monotonic progression within the already elevated range.That interpretation should still remain bounded. The current report does not prove that depression is fundamentally a reachability disorder or a 9D Jacobian disorder. It shows only that, in this dataset and export surface, those layers carry the clearest BDI-linked organization, while the coarser 3D summaries do not.LimitationsThis manuscript has several important limits.1.It is a single-dataset report in a young, age-compressed resting-state cohort.2.The band sizes are imbalanced, especially for BDI 14-19 (n = 15), which can inflate uncertainty and effect-size volatility.3.The article is anchored primarily to BDI, not to a uniformly adjudicated diagnostic major depression label. Only part of the high-BDI subgroup has SCID and HamD annotation.4.The strongest bin effects are internally coherent, but they remain cohort-local findings rather than settled clinical thresholds.5.Several selected effects outside reachability, 9D MNJ, and distribution summaries are weak or non-significant after correction.ConclusionThe current ds003478 analysis supports a bounded conclusion: resting-state depressive symptom burden is detectable in a fixed NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 frame, but in this export 9Depressive Symptom Geometry10it localizes more clearly to reachability geometry, 9D local Jacobian dynamics, and related distri­bution summaries than to the selected 3D summaries. The strongest organization is a separation between low and elevated BDI regimes, while within-high-BDI gradients remain less stable. The aim is not to turn the paper into a stand-alone theory paper, but to make the reported summaries easier to read in clinically recognizable terms.The coarse 3D stateThe primary chart used throughout the manuscript is𝑥𝑡=[𝑚𝑡,𝑑𝑡,𝑒𝑡]𝑇.These three coordinates should be read as operational summary families rather than as direct measurements of one biological variable each.m: mobility / metastability family. In plain terms, m tracks how readily the signal appears to move through organized state-space structure. In this depression report, m contributes some first-order displacement, but it is not the dominant 3D separator.d: deviation / redistribution family. d indexes how far the system sits from its more typical balance of integration and segregation under the fixed measurement contract. In the present cohort, d is the weakest of the three coarse low-versus-high BDI axes.10Depressive Symptom Geometry11e: entropy / irregularity family. e summarizes complexity pressure, irregularity, and less compressible organization in the exported state representation. In this manuscript, the most visible 3D centroid shift is e-weighted rather than d-weighted.Axis guardrailThe m, d, and e axes are release-bound measurement families. They are not direct stand-ins for single symptom clusters, neurotransmitters, or one bedside depression score.The stratified 9D layerThe higher-resolution chart is𝑥9𝑡=[𝑚𝑎,𝑚𝑒,𝑚𝑜,𝑑𝑛,𝑑𝑙,𝑑𝑠,𝑒𝑒,𝑒𝑠,𝑒𝑚]𝑇.This layer decomposes the same trajectory into subfamilies. The practical reason for reporting it is that a weak coarse 3D effect can still hide a more specific higher-resolution displacement. In this depression cohort, the most visible first-order low-versus-high BDI shift localizes to e_m and m_o, rather than to a broad uniform displacement across all nine subcoordinates.Clinically, the 9D layer is best read as a localization device inside the state-space rather than as a set of nine independent biomarkers. It helps answer questions such as: is the visible shift broad, or is it concentrated in one specific mobility, deviation, or entropy subfamily?First-order geometry versus local dynamicsThe manuscript reports several layers because they answer different questions:•3D / 9D MNPS: where subjects tend to sit in state space.•MNJ: how the local flow tends to expand, contract, rotate, or redirect.•Block-MNJ: which broad cross-family couplings may contribute to that local redirection.•Reachability: how locally accessible or constrained nearby dynamical space appears to be.11Depressive Symptom Geometry12From a reader’s perspective, the most important distinction is between location and movement. The safest clinical reading is narrower.13Depressive Symptom Geometry14First, the paper suggests that depressive symptom burden in this cohort is not well summa­rized by one universal coarse scalar. A low-versus-high BDI separation is visible, but its first-order geometry is weak in 3D and clearest in specific 9D subfamilies, especially e_m_mean, with parallel but less correction-stable support in e_m_median and m_o.Second, BDI should not be conflated with a uniformly adjudicated major depression diagnosis here. The present report is anchored primarily to symptom burden rather than to a frozen diagnostic case-control label, and only part of the high-BDI subgroup has SCID or HamD annotation.Third, one clinically readable way to summarize the reachability result is that high-BDI recordings appear to occupy a different short-horizon dynamic regime. In this cohort, the strong reachability pattern, including the convergent tube_log_det_mean difference, should not be read as “better” or “healthier” capacity, but as evidence that the local neighborhood of reachable next states is configured differently in the elevated-symptom group.Fourth, the centroid-adjusted appendix matters clinically because it narrows the strongest claim. It suggests that some of the apparent global 9D / reachability difference reflects where high-BDI recordings sit in the shared state-space, while the strongest residual difference after adjustment is more localized in block-level coupling than in a broad global dynamic shift.Fifth, none of the results here are ready for individual-level clinical use. The analysis is group-level, the middle BDI band is small, and the manuscript does not establish a bedside biomarker, prognostic score, or treatment-response rule.Clinical bottom lineIn cautious clinical terms, the paper’s main result is that elevated depressive symptom burden is geometrically visible in resting EEG, including a reachability shift that suggests a 14Depressive Symptom Geometry15different local dynamic neighborhood. But the dominant organization in this cohort is still best read as a modest coarse displacement, a clearer stratified 9D displacement centered most cleanly on e_m_mean, and only more selective residual local-dynamical differences after centroid adjustment.AcknowledgementsThis work emerged through structured correspondence among human and AI collaborators; large language models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google assisted in literature synthesis, drafting, peer review, and editorial refinement.
