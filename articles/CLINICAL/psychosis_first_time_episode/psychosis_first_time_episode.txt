First-Episode Psychosis Geometry Across Two Resting-State EEG CohortsCompressed standalone manuscript draftNeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 / Noetic Diffusion Theory analysis of ds003944 and ds003947AbstractWe analysed two resting-state EEG cohorts of first-episode psychosis using NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 within a Noetic Diffusion Theory (NDT) framework to estimate low-dimensional neural-state trajectories and local dynamics for each subject in a shared cross-subject reference frame. The primary design compared three groups in each cohort: Healthy, FEP unmedicated, and FEP medicated, with CPZ_at_scan > 0 treated as medicated and CPZ_at_scan = 0 or n/a treated as unmedicated. Missing CPZ values were minimal (n = 2 in ds003944, n = 0 in ds003947), and control/FEP age means were broadly similar within each dataset. In ds003944 (32 healthy, 19 unmedicated, 31 medicated), the strongest effects were concentrated in the global and stratified m-axis for FEP medicated vs Healthy, with BH-FDR surviving hits for m_mean (𝑑=0.881, 𝑝=0.0015, 𝑞=0.0090), m_median (𝑑=0.865, 𝑝=0.0020, 𝑞=0.0090), and stratified m_o_mean (𝑑=0.814, 𝑝=0.0030, 𝑞=0.032). In the same cohort, FEP medicated vs FEP unmedicated also separated on m_std (𝑑=0.972, 𝑝=0.0010, 𝑞=0.0090). In ds003947 (30 healthy, 8 unmedicated, 23 medicated), the most robust signal shifted from static group means to dynamics: FEP unmedicated vs Healthy showed a q-significant reduction in trajectory m_delta_mean_median (𝑑=−1.385, 𝑝=0.0015, 𝑞=0.030), while 9D MNJ and block-MNJ layers contributed uncorr-sig or trend-level support. A pooled Healthy vs FEP sensitivity test confirmed a stable psychosis signal in ds003944, but partially diluted the ds003947 abnormal-dynamics effect. Exploratory FEP-only phenotype analyses added clinical context: higher hallucination burden (SAPS_Q7) mapped most clearly onto an e-axis displacement in ds003944, whereas higher overall psychiatric burden (BPRST19) was associated with stronger first-order and block-Jacobian effects. Across cohorts, the same NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 workflow yielded readable medication-stratified compar­isons, but the locus of separation differed by cohort: ds003944 emphasized stable m-axis displacement in medicated FEP, whereas ds003947 emphasized abnormal m-trajectory dynamics in unmedicated FEP.IntroductionFirst-episode psychosis is unlikely to be captured by a single geometric signature. Clinical state, medication exposure, and cohort composition can all redistribute the observable signal across first-order coordinates, higher-dimensional substructure, and Jacobian-derived dynamics. A useful analysis framework therefore has to do two things at once: preserve interpretable subject summaries and place recordings in a common reference frame for between-subject contrasts. More broadly, psychosis has long been framed as a disorder of disturbed functional integration and dysconnectivity rather than of isolated regional abnormality (Pet­tersson-Yeo et al., 2011; Stephan et al., 2009). Existing EEG and clinical psychosis literatures already point toward altered microstate dynamics, aberrant salience, alpha-band dysfunction, and abnormal complexity, but they do not yet provide one unified low-dimensional dynamical chart (Kapur, 2003; Palaniyappan & Liddle, 2012; Rieger et al., 2016; Takahashi, 2013).Resting EEG studies also suggest that relevant abnormalities are already visible near illness onset. Clas­sical qEEG work found increased low-frequency activity and reduced alpha in first-episode schizophrenia, while later FEP studies linked resting spectral abnormalities to 1-year symptomatic and functional outcome (Renaldi et al., 2019; Sponheim et al., 1994).Here we use NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1, an NDT-aligned pipeline that converts each resting EEG recording into a trajectory in a low-dimensional neural state space together with local Jacobian estimates (Langell, 2025a; R. Langell, 2026; R. C. Langell, 2026). In practice, this gives one reproducible set of 3D coordinates, one higher-resolution 9D decomposition, and one set of local dynamical summaries per subject (Langell, 2025b; 2025c). The present manuscript applies that workflow to two psychosis cohorts, ds003944 and ds003947, and asks a focused question: when we split FEP into medicated and unmedicated groups, where do the reproducible cohort-level separations appear?This is not a benchmark across multiple normalization schemes. Instead, it is a paper-facing demonstra­tion of one reproducible NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 analysis pathway: three-group contrasts, common-effect summaries, and shared figures interpreted in one cross-subject state space.MethodsDatasets and group definitionBoth datasets were analysed from the 2026-05-20 NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 runs. The figure emphasizes how ds003944 concentrates separation in static and stratified m metrics for medicated FEP, whereas ds003947 places its strongest corrected effect in trajectory m_delta_mean_median for unmedicated FEP.DiscussionThis compressed study supports three cautious conclusions.First, the NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 framework is useful for the question we asked. It makes three-group psychosis contrasts straightforward to compute and summarize within one shared state-space definition across subjects.Second, medication stratification is not a cosmetic refinement. In ds003944, the medicated subgroup carries the strongest and cleanest m-axis displacement, although the pooled sensitivity test shows that a broader psychosis signal still survives in global and stratified m features, especially m_e. In ds003947, the unmedicated subgroup instead carries the only q-significant dynamic effect, and the pooled sensitivity test weakens that result, which argues directly against collapsing medication states too early. This medication-linked redistribution is at least qualitatively compatible with prior EEG and neurocognitive reports that antipsychotic exposure can stabilize some dimensions of pathological activity while introducing a different kind of rigidity (Centorrino et al., 2002; Keefe et al., 2007).Third, the m axis remains important, but not always in the same form. In one cohort it appears as a stable global displacement (m_mean, m_median, m_std), while in the other it appears most clearly as an altered trajectory drift term (m_delta_mean_median). That pattern is consistent with a geometry-first view in which the same NDT-aligned state-space exposes a common family of psychosis-sensitive coordinates, but the exact feature expression remains cohort-dependent. The safest oscillatory interpretation is therefore not that m measures one single rhythm, but that it reflects altered rhythm-weighted coordination whose expression can shift between static occupancy and dynamic instability, broadly in line with early-psychosis gamma and ASSR abnormalities reported in MEG and electrophysiology meta-analyses (Grent-'t-Jong & others, 2018; Zouaoui & others, 2023).The present EEG findings also fit naturally within a broader multimodal dysconnectivity literature. Resting-state fMRI studies in first-episode and early psychosis have reported altered default-mode, salience-network, and wider cortical-subcortical-cerebellar connectivity patterns, suggesting that the cohort-level EEG geometry reported here may be one modality-specific expression of a broader network disturbance (Alonso-Solís & others, 2012; Huang et al., 2022; Jensen & others, 2024).The main limitations are equally clear. ds003947 has only eight unmedicated participants, so second-order and block-level effects there should be treated as supportive rather than definitive. We also did not benchmark alternative preprocessing or manifold-construction schemes in the present paper, so the current results should be read as evidence for one reproducible NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 workflow rather than as a universal proof that any single implementation choice is optimal. Finally, block-MNJ remains exploratory in both cohorts: it adds readable structure, but the strongest block-level findings do not survive family-wise FDR here. The pooled sensitivity analysis helps on one reviewer-facing point, however: because only two FEP participants across both cohorts had missing CPZ values, the difference between pooled and stratified results is unlikely to be driven by missingness alone. The phenotype analyses add clinical texture but do not fully remove confounding by medication state, especially for the hallucination split, so they are best read as hypothesis-generating extensions rather than as replacement primary endpoints. More generally, early-psychosis electrophysiology is heterogeneous even for comparatively established markers such as mismatch negativity, where chronic schizophrenia findings are stronger and first-episode effects remain more debated (Haigh et al., 2017; Tada et al., 2019).
