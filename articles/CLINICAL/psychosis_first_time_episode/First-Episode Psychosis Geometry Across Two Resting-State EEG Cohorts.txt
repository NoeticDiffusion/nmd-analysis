Article type: Research Article / Methods-Anchored Empirical ReportFirst-Episode Psychosis Geometry Across Two Resting-State EEG CohortsNeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 / Noetic Diffusion Theory analysis of ds003944 and ds003947Short title: Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG CohortsRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenCorresponding author: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comKeywords: first-episode psychosis, EEG, NeuralManifoldDynamics, MNPS, MNJ, medication stratificationAbstractWe analysed two resting-state EEG cohorts of first-episode psychosis using NeuralManifoldDy­namics 2.1 within a Noetic Diffusion Theory (NDT) framework to estimate low-dimensional neural-state trajectories and local dynamics for each subject in one shared cross-subject reference frame. The primary design compared Healthy, FEP unmedicated, and FEP medicated, with CPZ_at_scan > 0 treated as medicated and CPZ_at_scan = 0 or n/a treated as unmedicated. The intended replication target was not one universal feature-level biomarker, but whether medication-stratified psychosis separation remained visible across cohorts under the same shared-reference analysis surface. In OpenNeuro dataset ds003944 (32 healthy, 19 unmedicated, 31 medicated), the strongest corrected effects were stable first-order and stratified m-family shifts in FEP medicated vs Healthy, including global m_mean (𝑑=0.881, 𝑞=0.0157), global m_median (𝑑=0.865, 𝑞=1Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG Cohorts20.0090), and stratified m_o_mean (𝑑=0.814, 𝑞=0.0270). In OpenNeuro dataset ds003947 (30 healthy, 8 unmedicated, 23 medicated), the strongest corrected effect instead appeared in dynamics: FEP unmedicated vs Healthy showed reduced trajectory m_delta_mean_median (𝑑=−1.385, 𝑞=0.0400), while higher-order Jacobian support remained exploratory. A pooled Healthy vs FEP sensitivity test confirmed a stable psychosis signal in ds003944 but weakened the ds003947 dy­namic effect, supporting medication stratification rather than premature pooling. Reviewer-facing baseline comparators showed that some subgroup separation was also visible in simple spectral and complexity summaries, so the present report should be read as demonstrating an interpretable shared state-space analysis surface rather than clear superiority over conventional EEG. Because the ds003947 unmedicated subgroup was small (n = 8), that dynamic effect should be treated as a strong cohort-local signal requiring replication rather than as a settled subgroup biomarker. These results support a bounded conclusion: NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1 exposes medication-sensitive psychosis geometry across two FEP EEG cohorts, but the feature carrying the signal is cohort-dependent, so the framework is best treated as a reproducible shared-reference analysis surface rather than a universal FEP biomarker.IntroductionFirst-episode psychosis is unlikely to be captured by a single geometric signature. Clinical state, medication exposure, and cohort composition can all redistribute the observable signal across first-order coordinates, higher-dimensional substructure, and Jacobian-derived dynamics. A useful analysis framework therefore has to do two things at once: preserve interpretable subject summaries and place recordings in a common reference frame for between-subject contrasts. More broadly, 2Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG Cohorts3psychosis has long been framed as a disorder of disturbed functional integration and dysconnec­tivity rather than of isolated regional abnormality (Pettersson-Yeo et al., 2011; Stephan et al., 2009). Existing EEG and clinical psychosis literatures already point toward altered microstate dynamics, aberrant salience, alpha-band dysfunction, and abnormal complexity, but they do not yet provide one unified low-dimensional dynamical chart (Kapur, 2003; Palaniyappan & Liddle, 2012; Rieger et al., 2016; Takahashi, 2013).Resting EEG studies also suggest that relevant abnormalities are already visible near illness onset. Classical qEEG work found increased low-frequency activity and reduced alpha in first-episode schizophrenia, while later FEP studies linked resting spectral abnormalities to 1-year symptomatic and functional outcome (Renaldi et al., 2019; Sponheim et al., 1994).Here we use NeuralManifoldDynamics 2.1, an NDT-aligned pipeline that converts each resting EEG recording into a trajectory in a low-dimensional neural state space together with local Jacobian estimates (Langell, 2025a; R. Langell, 2026; R. C. Langell, 2026). In practice, this gives one reproducible set of 3D coordinates, one higher-resolution 9D decomposition, and one set of local dynamical summaries per subject (Langell, 2025b; 2025c). The present manuscript applies that workflow to two psychosis cohorts, ds003944 and ds003947, and asks a focused question: when we split FEP into medicated and unmedicated groups, where do the reproducible cohort-level separations appear? The intended replication target is not one exact feature, but whether medication-stratified psychosis separation remains visible in a fixed shared-reference state space across cohorts. The aim is not to replace established EEG markers, but to test whether a fixed shared-reference state-space can organize them into interpretable first-order, trajectory-level, and local-dynamical summaries.3Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG Cohorts4This is not a benchmark across multiple normalization schemes. In practical terms, it makes three-group psychosis contrasts straightforward to compute and summarize within one common state-space definition across subjects. That is the paper’s real replication target: not exact feature identity, but reproducible medication-sensitive separability under one locked analysis surface.Second, medication stratification is not a cosmetic refinement. In ds003944, the medicated subgroup carries the strongest and cleanest m-axis displacement, although the pooled sensitivity test shows that a broader psychosis signal still survives in global and stratified m features, especially m_e. In ds003947, the unmedicated subgroup instead carries the only q-significant dynamic effect, and the pooled sensitivity test weakens that result, which argues directly against collapsing medication states too early. This medication-linked redistribution is at least qualitatively compatible with prior EEG and neurocognitive reports that antipsychotic exposure can stabilize some dimensions of pathological activity while introducing a different kind of rigidity (Centorrino et al., 2002; Keefe et al., 2007). At the same time, the ds003944 medicated-FEP separation should not be read as “the psychosis signal”: it may reflect medication exposure, illness severity correlated with treatment, clinical selection, or a medication-by-illness interaction.Third, the m axis remains important, but not always in the same form. In one cohort it appears as a stable global displacement (m_mean, m_median, m_std), while in the other it appears most clearly as an altered trajectory drift term (m_delta_mean_median). That pattern is consistent with a geometry-first view in which the same NDT-aligned state-space exposes a common family 17Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG Cohorts18of psychosis-sensitive coordinates, but the exact feature expression remains cohort-dependent. The safest oscillatory interpretation is therefore not that m measures one single rhythm, but that it reflects altered rhythm-weighted coordination whose expression can shift between static occupancy and dynamic instability, broadly in line with early-psychosis gamma and ASSR abnormalities reported in MEG and electrophysiology meta-analyses (Grent-'t-Jong & others, 2018; Zouaoui & others, 2023). The reviewer-facing comparator panel reinforces the bounded version of that claim: simple spectral and complexity baselines capture part of the subgroup separation, so the present work should be read as an organizing analysis surface rather than as a definitive superiority benchmark.The present EEG findings also fit naturally within a broader multimodal dysconnectivity literature. Resting-state fMRI studies in first-episode and early psychosis have reported altered default-mode, salience-network, and wider cortical-subcortical-cerebellar connectivity patterns, suggesting that the cohort-level EEG geometry reported here may be one modality-specific expres­sion of a broader network disturbance (Alonso-Solís & others, 2012; Huang et al., 2022; Jensen & others, 2024). At the same time, scalp EEG does not have the spatial resolution to claim direct network-specific mechanism from these coordinates alone; the most defensible wording is that the exported geometry is compatible with broader dysconnectivity models, not that it localizes them.The main limitations are equally clear. ds003947 has only eight unmedicated participants, so second-order and block-level effects there should be treated as supportive rather than defin­itive. We also did not benchmark alternative preprocessing or manifold-construction schemes exhaustively, so the current results should be read as evidence for one reproducible NeuralMani­foldDynamics 2.1 workflow rather than as a universal proof that any single implementation choice is optimal. Relatedly, the reported q-values control within contrast-family blocks rather than across the full manuscript-wide metric universe. Block-MNJ remains exploratory in both cohorts: it 18Psychosis Geometry Across Two EEG Cohorts19adds readable structure, but the strongest block-level findings do not survive family-wise FDR here and should not be overread as specific circuit claims. The pooled sensitivity analysis helps on one reviewer-facing point, however: because only two FEP participants across both cohorts had missing CPZ values, the difference between pooled and stratified results is unlikely to be driven by missingness alone. The phenotype analyses add clinical texture but do not fully remove confounding by medication state, especially for the hallucination split, so they are best read as hypothesis-generating extensions rather than as replacement primary endpoints. More generally, early-psychosis electrophysiology is heterogeneous even for comparatively established markers such as mismatch negativity, where chronic schizophrenia findings are stronger and first-episode effects remain more debated (Haigh et al., 2017; Tada et al., 2019).
