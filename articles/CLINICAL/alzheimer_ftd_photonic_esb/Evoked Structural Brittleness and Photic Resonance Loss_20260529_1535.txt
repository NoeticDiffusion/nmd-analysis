Article type: Research Article / Follow-up Working PaperEvoked Structural Brittleness as a Perturbational Geometry of NeurodegenerationA ds004504-Anchored Photic EEG Study in Alzheimer Disease and Frontotemporal DementiaShort title: Photic ESBRobin Langell11 Langell Konsult AB, Vallentuna, SwedenCorresponding author: Robin Langell, hello@noeticdiffusion.comKeywords: Alzheimer disease, frontotemporal dementia, photic stimulation, EEG, Evoked Structural Brittleness, NeuralManifoldDynamicsAbstractIn this internally stabilized follow-up analysis, we built a standalone article-local workflow around the ds006036 photic EEG cohort and subject-matched ds004504 resting baselines to test whether neurodegenerative groups show abnormal evoked deformation relative to a healthy response template. Using an exploratory-frozen ESB-2 specification, the primary score combined parietal-occipital tube_d_eff_median with global rotational_power_median after subject-level ds004504 baseline subtraction. The clearest group separation localized to PHOTO 15 Hz: AD versus Healthy reached 𝑑=0.648 and 𝑞=0.0239, while FTD versus Healthy reached 𝑑=0.922 and 𝑞=0.0041. Cross-fitted Healthy rescoring preserved the main PHOTO 15 Hz effect, though with smaller magnitudes (𝑑=0.565 for AD versus Healthy and 𝑑=0.815 for FTD versus Healthy). 1Photic ESB2Decomposition showed that the deviation was mainly capacity-led rather than guidance-led. A conventional comparator panel based on posterior band-power, alpha power, alpha/theta ratio, and sample entropy showed some partial overlap with the group signal, but its association with ESB-2 remained weak and did not survive correction at the article level. Leave-one-out and permutation QC supported the PHOTO 15 Hz ESB-2 peak. A secondary profile-based PRL test did not yield a stable article-level discriminator, and recovery-weighted variants remained bounded by the eyes-closed versus eyes-open state mismatch between ds004504 and the post-photic residual surface. The bounded conclusion is that ESB-2 is an internally stabilized, disease-versus-Healthy pertur­bational deviation score on this cohort, not an externally validated biomarker or resonance-loss theorem.IntroductionThe earlier photic EEG article in this repository established a narrow but corrected AD-versus-Healthy signal on a global Meta-Noetic Jacobian surface and showed that the overlapping resting-state reference was much weaker at baseline [1], [2], [3]. That result was intentionally compact and single-metric. The present follow-up asks a different question: can the same cohort support a perturbational deviation score defined relative to a healthy evoked response template rather than a single exported endpoint?Rhythmic visual forcing is a natural test bed for that question. Human EEG responses to photic flicker can entrain ongoing oscillations and reveal frequency-specific response structure that is not obvious in passive baseline recordings [4], [5]. This is clinically relevant because Alzheimer disease and behavioural-variant frontotemporal dementia do not primarily damage the 2Photic ESB3same systems: AD more strongly emphasizes posterior and temporoparietal vulnerability, whereas bvFTD preferentially affects frontal-insular and salience-network organization [6], [7], [8].This manuscript centers on one empirical score: Evoked Structural Brittleness, abbreviated ESB-2. ESB-2 measures how far a subject’s rest-to-photic response vector deviates from the Healthy template in a two-axis space formed by posterior reachability capacity and global dynam­ical guidance. A secondary profile-based extension, Photic Resonance Loss, was also evaluated, but it is not treated as a co-equal headline finding because it did not stabilize as an article-level discriminator on the current four-frequency surface.For readers outside the broader Noetic Diffusion framework, the analysis can be read operationally as follows: windowed EEG dynamics were summarized on a fixed low-dimensional measurement contract; subject-level photic-stage summaries were compared to subject-matched resting baselines; and group differences were tested on these exported measurements. The present claims concern only those exported measurements and their article-local audit trail, not the full theoretical scope of Noetic Diffusion Theory [9] or the full NeuralManifoldDynamics interpreta­tion [10].The paper is therefore bounded by three questions:1.Does ds004504-anchored ESB-2 produce reproducible disease-versus-healthy separation on the frozen photic surface?2.If so, is the deviation mainly capacity-led, guidance-led, or both?3.Do secondary profile or recovery extensions add stable information beyond the core ESB-2 result?3Photic ESB4MethodsDatasets and comparability contractThe analysis couples the ds006036 eyes-open photic cohort [11] with the ds004504 resting-state cohort [12]. Subject matching was performed by subject ID, and subject-level ds004504 baselines were available for all 88 photic participants. Ds004504 is a resting, eyes-closed baseline dataset, whereas the ds006036 analysis surface is eyes-open and stimulus-evoked. The main row was prefigured by the exploratory pass, was recovered by the article-local build, remained stable under leave-one-out template perturbation, and retained low empirical permutation p-values. That is enough to claim that a rest-anchored capacity-guidance deviation score can be measured on this cohort.Second, the score changes the disease story relative to the narrower MNJ paper. The older mdr_median line was strongest for AD versus Healthy and peaked at PHOTO 10 Hz. The present ESB-2 line peaks at PHOTO 15 Hz and separates both AD and FTD from Healthy. Composite deviation is therefore broader than the earlier single-metric signal, but also less diagnosis-specific. In practical terms, ESB-2 looks more like a disease-versus-Healthy perturbational score than an AD-only marker.Third, the decomposition matters. The strongest signed movement is capacity-led: both disease groups show more negative posterior Delta C than Healthy, especially around PHOTO 10 and 15 Hz. Guidance contributes additional spread, but it is not independently convincing on this surface. That makes the current article more about abnormal response geometry than about a pure rotational-collapse narrative.Fourth, the comparator panel makes the interpretation more credible. Some ordinary poste­rior measures, especially alpha-linked power change, do show disease-versus-Healthy separation on the same rest-subtracted surface. But their relationship to ESB-2 is weak and becomes near-null within the disease-only subset. That suggests that ESB-2 partly overlaps with familiar posterior photic response changes without collapsing to a single conventional measure. Because exact 16Photic ESB17narrowband stimulation-frequency amplitude was not available on the frozen feature surface, this remains a broad posterior control rather than a definitive entrainment control.Fifth, PHOTO 15 Hz should not be overread. It is the strongest observed frequency on the present four-frequency surface and survives a small frequency-only audit within the primary specification, but that does not make it a confirmed resonance peak. The negative PRL result is especially important here because it argues against a stable frequency-profile claim.Sixth, the negative PRL result is still useful. A four-point correlation-based profile score was simple and auditable, but it did not survive as an article-level discriminator. The current data therefore support a strong stage-local perturbational score more clearly than a resonance-loss theorem. A future PRL revision may still succeed, but it would likely require denser frequency support or a different profile summary.LimitationsSix limitations define the present claim boundary.1.The article’s central comparison uses a resting, eyes-closed ds004504 anchor against an eyes-open, stimulus-evoked ds006036 surface. That is suitable for subject-aligned baseline subtraction, but not for a state-matched recovery theorem.2.The primary ESB-2 specification was selected from exploratory work before the article-local rerun. The present manuscript therefore reports an internally stabilized development-stage metric rather than an externally validated one.3.The planned global-capacity sensitivity specification could not be realized on the frozen stage export because stage-wise global reachability rows were absent from the exported metric surface.17Photic ESB184.PRL was tested over only four frequencies and did not stabilize as a corrected group discrimi­nator. It therefore remains secondary.5.The comparator panel is limited to exported broad-band and entropy features. Exact narrow­band stimulation-frequency SSVEP amplitude was not available on the frozen contract, so the nearest-band posterior power proxy should be read as an approximation rather than as a direct entrainment estimate.6.Coverage drops at PHOTO 20 Hz, especially in FTD, so the article centers its inference on the better-supported PHOTO 15 Hz peak and cross-frequency disease profiles should not be interpreted as complete response curves.Within those limits, the new paper still shows something concrete: disease groups deviate from the Healthy photic response template more strongly than Healthy controls do, and the cleanest departure localizes to a posterior-capacity-led composite rather than to a simple resting-state baseline deficit or a single conventional posterior comparator.ConclusionThis follow-up article supports a bounded perturbational-deviation claim. On the validated ds006036 plus ds004504 surface, ESB-2 is reproducibly elevated in both AD and FTD relative to Healthy, with the clearest shared peak at PHOTO 15 Hz. The signal is capacity-led, remains present under cross-fitted Healthy rescoring, and is stronger than a null result. At the same time, the current data describe an internally stabilized development-stage metric rather than an externally validated biomarker, and they do not justify broader claims about resonance loss or recovery failure.
