### IQR/MAD: yes, and there is already a strong reason to test it

Your existing coma manuscript already found that **between-recording variability can separate CPC1/CPC5 more strongly than the corresponding median**. For example, between-run MAD of `tube_capture_gate_median` reached \(d=-1.03\), and `persistence_hysteresis_log_det_median` MAD also separated the groups; CPC5 was the more variable group. 

So I would open a new line:

$$
\boxed{\text{COMA-L1-STAB-001}}
$$

asking whether the new dense L1 dynamics are not only shifted but **less temporally stable in CPC5**.

There are actually two different variability scales worth keeping separate:

$$
V^{\rm within}_m(i)
=
\operatorname{median}_{r}
\left[
IQR_t(m_{irt})
\right]
$$

measures fluctuation *inside recordings*, whereas

$$
V^{\rm between}_m(i)
=
MAD_r
\left[
\operatorname{median}_t(m_{irt})
\right]
$$

measures instability *between recordings* for the same patient.

I would use **between-run MAD as primary**, because you already have an independent internal reason to care about that scale, and IQR as the robustness companion. IQR is excellent, but MAD is slightly less sensitive to a few pathological runs.

### Another metric I now like more than \(d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}\)

Since transverse dimensionality was null, ask instead whether mean drift aligns with the **dominant spread direction**.

Define

$$
\boxed{
A_{\rm dom}
=
\frac{
u_b^\top a u_b
}{
\lambda_{\max}(a)
}
}
$$

with

$$
0\le A_{\rm dom}\le1.
$$

This is effectively a normalized Rayleigh quotient.

* \(A_{\rm dom}\approx1\): drift points along the major conditional-spread axis.
* \(A_{\rm dom}\ll1\): systematic motion and the major spread direction are geometrically dissociated.

This is different from

$$
F_\parallel
=
\frac{u_b^\top a u_b}{\operatorname{tr}(a)}
$$

because \(F_\parallel\) is affected by how broadly total covariance is distributed across all eigenmodes. \(A_{\rm dom}\) asks more directly:

> **Is the deterministic local corridor aligned with the dominant stochastic/conditional corridor?**

Given the DDC result, a plausible CPC1 prediction would be

$$
A_{\rm dom,CPC1}<A_{\rm dom,CPC5}.
$$

I would call it something like:

`conditional_mean_dominant_spread_alignment_level1`.

This should remain exploratory until truth-known tests pass.

## Combining with Jacobian metrics: yes, but not the old way

Here I would draw a hard line.

The uploaded coma manuscript still reports strong 3D/9D MNJ spectral-radius and Frobenius effects and interprets CPC1 as having lower local Jacobian deformation.  But our later September qualification work showed that the current I-CARE chart does **not broadly license the continuous-generator/Family-B interpretation**.

So I would **not create a new CPC score that mixes \(G_{90}\), \(R_{b/a}\), and the old spectral radius or \(\alpha\)**.

That would mix:

$$
\text{dense, qualified L1 observations}
$$

with

$$
\text{a much more weakly supported operator layer}.
$$

Instead I would combine the new metrics with the **qualified Level-2 discrete operator family**, which has vastly better support than Family-B \(\alpha\).

The particularly natural pair is:

$$
g^{\rm obs}_{90}
=
\frac{\log G_{90}}{\Delta t}
$$

versus

$$
g^{\rm op}_{\max}
=
\frac{\log\sigma_{\max}(\Phi)}{\Delta t}.
$$

Both have rate units and ask related but distinct questions:

* \(g^{\rm obs}_{90}\): upper-tail *actually observed* same-pair amplification;
* \(g^{\rm op}_{\max}\): maximal amplification predicted by the OOS-qualified discrete affine map.

I would **not immediately subtract them and call the remainder “noise.”** But a cross-level diagnostic

$$
\Delta g
=
g^{\rm obs}_{90}-g^{\rm op}_{\max}
$$

could be useful as an exploratory **model-gap** quantity:

`observed_operator_amplification_gap`.

Positive values would mean observed q90 separation exceeds what the fitted one-step linear operator predicts; that could reflect stochasticity, nonlinearity, hidden state, model misspecification, or combinations thereof.

Not “diffusion.”

### A cleaner combined experiment

I think a very good later test would be:

$$
\boxed{\text{COMA-L1L2-001}}
$$

with the scientific question:

> Is recovery-linked amplification primarily visible in observed local trajectories, in the deterministic one-step operator, or in their mismatch?

Use three quantities per subject:

$$
g^{\rm obs}_{90},
\qquad
g^{\rm op}_{\max},
\qquad
\Delta g.
$$

Then we can get qualitatively different regimes:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
g^{\rm obs}_{90} & g^{\rm op}_{\max} & \text{reading}\\
\hline
\uparrow & \uparrow & deterministic/operator amplification\\
\uparrow & \approx & excess observed amplification\\
\approx & \uparrow & latent operator potential not realized\\
\downarrow & \downarrow & broadly quieter dynamics
\end{array}
$$

That gives much more mechanistic information than putting Jacobian spectral radius into a regression beside \(G_{90}\).

And because L2 has roughly 85–86% coverage rather than 1–3%, this is at least potentially a population-level analysis—subject to repeating the same missingness audit you did for L1.
