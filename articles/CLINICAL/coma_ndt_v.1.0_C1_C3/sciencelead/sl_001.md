Yes. I think I-CARE is probably the **best dataset to demonstrate why the new level system is useful**, because the strongest high-level local operator object—spectral abscissa—has become method-limited, while the dataset clearly still contains recovery-linked dynamical structure.

The existing I-CARE result already gives us a useful prior: later CPC1 patients showed broader effective reachability dimensionality, while the older Jacobian layer suggested lower local expansion/deformation than CPC5.  That combination suggests a very specific question for the new Level 0–1 layer:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can CPC1 retain broader local directional freedom without showing stronger local divergence?}
}
$$

That is much more interesting than simply asking whether CPC1 has “more dynamics.”

## The phenotype I would test

Very provisionally, I would expect the recoverable state to look like

$$
\boxed{
\text{CPC1: broad, multidirectional, but bounded}
}
$$

versus something closer to

$$
\boxed{
\text{CPC5: narrower/canalized and/or locally more divergent}
}
$$

That interpretation is consistent with the existing finding that effective reachability dimension was higher in CPC1 while Jacobian spectral-radius/Frobenius summaries were lower. 

But now we can test the two pieces **without requiring \(J\)**.

---

## I would start with about six primary Level 0–1 measurements

### 1. `realized_speed_level0`

$$
v_t
=
\frac{\|x_{t+1}-x_t\|}{\Delta t}.
$$

Question:

> Is CPC5 simply moving less/more through the chart?

This is mostly a descriptive anchor. I would not expect this alone to tell the full story.

It lets us distinguish:

$$
\text{low accessibility}
$$

from merely

$$
\text{slow trajectory}.
$$

---

### 2. `turning_rate_level0`

$$
\theta_t
=
\arccos
\frac{
\Delta x_t^\top\Delta x_{t+1}
}{
\|\Delta x_t\|\|\Delta x_{t+1}\|
}.
$$

Question:

> Does the trajectory retain directional flexibility, or does it continue along a narrow groove?

A plausible hypothesis is that CPC1 has more **structured directional reconfiguration**, whereas CPC5 could be more straight/canalized or noisy/random depending on subtype.

I would **not preregister the sign yet**. Turning can be high because of rich dynamics or because of noise.

But combined with diffusion dimensionality it becomes informative.

---

## 3. `conditional_mean_rate_level1`

This gives the first genuinely local drift-like quantity:

$$
\hat b_\Delta(x)
=
\frac1{\Delta t}
E[\Delta X\mid X_t\approx x].
$$

Then at minimum calculate:

$$
\|\hat b_\Delta(x)\|.
$$

Call it:

```text
conditional_mean_rate_norm_level1
```

This tells us:

> How strong is the systematic local tendency after averaging away part of the stochastic fluctuation?

I would **not currently predict CPC1 > CPC5 or vice versa**.

A more interesting possibility is that the magnitude doesn't separate much at all.

That would be informative if diffusion geometry does.

You could then get:

$$
\|\hat b\|_{CPC1}
\approx
\|\hat b\|_{CPC5}
$$

but

$$
d_{\rm eff}^{CPC1}
>
d_{\rm eff}^{CPC5}.
$$

That would mean recovery is less about “more movement” and more about the geometry of available variation.

---

# 4. This one is probably the most important: `diffusion_effective_dimension_level1`

From

$$
a_\Delta(x)
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta X\mid X_t\approx x)
}{
\Delta t
},
$$

take eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) and compute

$$
\boxed{
d_{\rm eff}^{(a)}
=
\frac{
(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2
}{
\sum_i\lambda_i^2
}.
}
$$

This is the Level-1 analogue I am most excited about for I-CARE.

The current reachability result already says that CPC1 has broader short-horizon effective dimensionality. In the corrected bundle, `tube_d_eff_median` was higher in CPC1 with a pooled effect around \(d=0.64\). 

Now we can ask something more fundamental:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Is that dimensionality already present in the local stochastic increments?}
}
$$

If yes:

$$
d_{\rm eff}^{(a)}(CPC1)
>
d_{\rm eff}^{(a)}(CPC5),
$$

then the reachability result may be largely rooted in **innovation/diffusion geometry**.

If not, but propagated reachability still differs, then the effect emerges from

$$
\text{diffusion}
+
\text{transport}.
$$

That would be a major mechanistic clarification.

---

# 5. `neighbor_separation_rate_level1`

This is the best low-assumption replacement for the scientific question we originally wanted spectral abscissa to answer.

Take nearby states \(x_i,x_j\), follow the **same pair** forward, and calculate

$$
\gamma_{ij}
=
\frac1{\Delta t}
\log
\frac{
\|x_{i+1}-x_{j+1}\|+\epsilon
}{
\|x_i-x_j\|+\epsilon
}.
$$

Then robustly aggregate.

The provisional directional hypothesis from the previous Jacobian result would be:

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm sep}^{CPC1}
<
\gamma_{\rm sep}^{CPC5}.
}
$$

That would mean:

> Locally similar recovery-linked trajectories remain more bounded over the next observed step.

This would be especially important because it could recover the **scientific content** of the old spectral-abscissa result without pretending we identified a generator.

If this shows the same CPC direction in discovery and confirmatory, I would take it much more seriously than the old sparse \(\alpha\).

---

# 6. `neighbor_gain_q90_level1`

Instead of mean separation, look at the upper tail:

$$
G_{ij}
=
\frac{d_{ij}(t+\Delta)}
{d_{ij}(t)+\epsilon},
$$

and

$$
G_{90}
=
Q_{0.90}(G_{ij}).
$$

This asks:

> How severe are the strongly amplifying local transitions?

My provisional prediction would again be:

$$
\boxed{
G_{90}^{CPC1}
<
G_{90}^{CPC5}.
}
$$

This may be even better than median separation if CPC5 contains occasional pathological explosive windows rather than globally higher expansion.

That would fit the idea that CPC5 is heterogeneous:

$$
\text{suppressed states}
\quad+\quad
\text{pathologically unstable/canalized states}.
$$

---

# 7. `cloud_volume_change_rate_level1`

This is particularly interesting because its direction is **not obvious**.

For a source neighbourhood covariance \(C_0\) and covariance \(C_1\) of the exact same trajectories one step later:

$$
g_{\rm cloud}
=
\frac{
\log\det(C_1+\epsilon I)
-
\log\det(C_0+\epsilon I)
}{
2\Delta t
}.
$$

Why is this useful?

Because we already have a potential dissociation:

CPC1 appears to have:

$$
\text{broader dimensionality}
$$

but

$$
\text{lower local expansion}.
$$

Those are not contradictory.

CPC1 could have spread distributed across several directions without rapidly increasing total volume.

For example:

$$
d_{\rm eff}\uparrow
\qquad
g_{\rm cloud}\approx0
$$

could mean:

> many locally available directions, but bounded total expansion.

Whereas CPC5 could show:

$$
d_{\rm eff}\downarrow
\qquad
g_{\rm cloud}\uparrow
$$

meaning:

> fewer dominant directions, but stronger deformation along them.

That would be a very compelling coma geometry phenotype.

---

# And now drift + diffusion together become especially interesting

Once Level-1 drift exists:

$$
\hat b_\Delta(x),
$$

and Level-1 diffusion exists:

$$
a_\Delta(x),
$$

we can compute a quantity we previously couldn't legitimately calculate.

Normalize drift:

$$
u_b
=
\frac{\hat b}{\|\hat b\|+\epsilon}.
$$

Then:

$$
D_{\parallel}
=
u_b^\top a u_b
$$

and

$$
D_{\perp}
=
\operatorname{tr}(a)-D_{\parallel}.
$$

Finally:

$$
\boxed{
f_\perp
=
\frac{D_\perp}
{\operatorname{tr}(a)+\epsilon}.
}
$$

This tells us:

> How much stochastic variation points **away from the current deterministic tendency**?

This may be extremely NDT-relevant.

A strong hypothesis would be:

$$
\boxed{
f_\perp^{CPC1}
>
f_\perp^{CPC5}.
}
$$

Meaning that recoverable brains retain more ways of moving **other than merely continuing their current trajectory**.

That is very close to the intuitive concept of accessibility.

But this should probably be a **secondary predefined endpoint**, because it combines two estimated Level-1 objects.

---

# There is also history

SL-006 already taught us something remarkable on the Type-C 0284 experiment:

$$
(z_t,z_{t-1})
$$

predicted the next state much better than \(z_t\) alone.

So we can add:

```text
history_predictive_gain_level1
```

$$
H_{\rm gain}
=
\operatorname{err}(M_0)
-
\operatorname{err}(M_1).
$$

Then ask:

$$
H_{\rm gain}^{CPC1}
\stackrel{?}{\neq}
H_{\rm gain}^{CPC5}.
$$

But I would **not make this primary immediately** because the Type-C history finding came from the specific 0284 development work.

It first needs to become a standardized measurement contract.

Still, biologically it asks something fascinating:

> Does good recovery preserve a richer dependence on recent trajectory history?

---

# The combined phenotype could become very powerful

Imagine the result looks like this:

| Metric                                 | CPC1 vs CPC5          |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| `realized_speed_level0`                | ~same                 |
| `conditional_mean_rate_norm_level1`    | ~same                 |
| `diffusion_effective_dimension_level1` | ↑ CPC1                |
| `drift_orthogonal_fraction_level1`     | ↑ CPC1                |
| `neighbor_separation_rate_level1`      | ↓ CPC1                |
| `neighbor_gain_q90_level1`             | ↓ CPC1                |
| `cloud_volume_change_rate_level1`      | ~same/slightly ↓ CPC1 |

Then the interpretation would be very specific:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Recovery is associated not with faster dynamics, but with broader yet better-bounded local possibilities.}
}
$$

That would essentially say:

CPC1:

$$
\text{more directions}
+
\text{less explosive amplification}
$$

CPC5:

$$
\text{fewer directions}
+
\text{greater local deformation/canalization}.
$$

That is a much richer result than:

> “spectral radius differs.”

And it closely matches the layered result already visible in the existing I-CARE analysis: broader predictive-spread dimensionality alongside lower local Jacobian deformation in later-good-outcome patients.

## I would make the first I-CARE experiment deliberately small

Since you have **Discovery** and **Confirmatory** cohorts, this is almost ideal.

I would freeze five primary Level-0/1 metrics on Discovery:

1. `conditional_mean_rate_norm_level1`
2. `diffusion_effective_dimension_level1`
3. `neighbor_separation_rate_level1`
4. `neighbor_gain_q90_level1`
5. `cloud_volume_change_rate_level1`

Then perhaps two secondary:

6. `diffusion_drift_orthogonal_fraction_level1`
7. `turning_rate_level0`

No spectral abscissa.

No 9D Jacobian.

No outcome-driven feature selection.

And most importantly, **don't use the confirmatory cohort to choose neighbourhood \(k\), epsilon, q90 vs q95, metric distance, or QC thresholds**.

Freeze those on truth-known + discovery, then run next_140 once.

---

## What I would consider the strongest possible result

Not necessarily five significant metrics.

The strongest result would be a **structured dissociation**:

$$
\boxed{
d_{\rm eff}\uparrow
\quad\text{while}\quad
\gamma_{\rm sep}\downarrow
}
$$

in CPC1, independently in discovery and confirmatory.

Because that would directly support:

$$
\boxed{
\text{breadth of possible local variation}
\neq
\text{local instability}.
}
$$

That is one of the core distinctions NDT is trying to make.

And unlike the old Family-B result, both quantities would be estimated at the level the I-CARE data actually appears capable of supporting.

One important caveat remains: CPC1 vs CPC5 is a **recovery-linked** contrast, not a pure consciousness or viability contrast, because treatment-limitation, time-to-death, and sedation information is incomplete in the manuscript-facing data. The NDT manuscript already explicitly maintains that boundary. 

So yes: I think the new Levels may actually make I-CARE **more scientifically informative**, not less. Instead of asking whether it can support one sophisticated local operator, we can map **which aspects of local dynamics survive empirically**—direction, diffusion breadth, local separation, amplification, and volume—and then see exactly how recovery changes that profile.
