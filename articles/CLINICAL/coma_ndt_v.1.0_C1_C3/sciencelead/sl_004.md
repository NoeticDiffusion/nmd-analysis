I think this is a **very good discovery result**, and more importantly it is scientifically cleaner than the old abscissa result. The two strongest findings form a coherent dissociation rather than five loosely significant metrics:

$$
\boxed{
d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(a)}\uparrow\ \text{in CPC1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
G_{90}\downarrow\ \text{in CPC1}
}
$$

while realized speed, turning, and history gain do not move appreciably. That points toward **broader local conditional spread with less upper-tail same-pair amplification**, not simply “more activity” or “faster dynamics.”

I would, however, change one phrase immediately: I would **not yet call \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(a)}\) “broader innovation geometry.”** In the H5 contract, `a_hat` is explicitly the centered covariance of raw conditional increments divided by \(\Delta t\), with `a_semantics=raw_increment_covariance`; it is *not* drift-residualized process noise. So the safe language is:

$$
\boxed{\text{broader conditional increment-covariance geometry}}
$$

or perhaps **broader local stochastic/conditional spread geometry**, with “innovation geometry” reserved for a future properly residualized object. The guide explicitly distinguishes `a_hat` from a residual innovation covariance and also warns that existing `d_diff` is not automatically the newly named `diffusion_effective_dimension_level1`. 

That distinction actually makes the result stronger epistemically: we do not have to pretend to have isolated an SDE innovation term.

## I would choose \(G_{90}\), not median separation, for part2

For the expansion/amplification side, I think the discovery evidence points pretty clearly toward:

$$
\boxed{\texttt{neighbor\_gain\_q90\_level1}}
$$

as the confirmatory metric.

There are three reasons. First, the effect is substantially larger:

$$
d=-0.63
$$

versus

$$
d=-0.27
$$

for median separation. Second, median separation gives somewhat discordant inferential evidence: the rank test is strong, but the Cohen-\(d\) interval crosses zero. That suggests the contrast may be distributional rather than well summarized by a mean-standardized shift. Third—and scientifically most interesting—the divergence seems concentrated in the **upper amplification tail** rather than being a uniform shift of the whole local-separation distribution.

That changes the biological reading. It is not:

> CPC5 trajectories generally diverge much faster.

It is more like:

> CPC5 has stronger high-amplification local episodes.

And the H5 definition is clean: \(G_{90}\) is the per-source 0.90 quantile of gains for the **same neighbor pairs followed one real step**, not an operator singular value, spectral abscissa, or peak propagator gain. The \(q=0.90\) value is already frozen in the contract. 

I would therefore make the part2 confirmatory pair:

$$
\boxed{
d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(a)}
\quad+\quad
G_{90}
}
$$

with median separation retained as a **pre-specified supportive endpoint**, not discarded.

The strongest replication would be that **both directions reproduce**:

$$
d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(a)}:\ CPC1>CPC5
$$

and

$$
G_{90}:\ CPC1<CPC5.
$$

I would probably use Holm across those two confirmatory endpoints, or define a fixed hierarchical test with \(d_{\mathrm{eff}}\) first and \(G_{90}\) second. Do not expand the confirmatory family further.

And I completely agree with not promoting cloud-volume after seeing \(p=0.0007\). Keep it discovery-only/exploratory. That is exactly the discipline part2 is meant to buy you.

# There are five things I would check before unlocking part2

These are more important than adding another metric.

| Audit                               | Why it matters                                                                                                                                      | My assessment                 |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Coordinate anchoring**            | L1 families are calculated on subject-anchored 3D, while the H5 guide says cohort-anchored coordinates are preferred for clinical group comparisons | **Important unresolved gate** |
| **\(a_{\Delta}\) semantics**        | `a_hat` is raw conditional increment covariance, not residual innovation                                                                            | Terminology correction needed |
| **\(G_{90}\) denominator/floor QC** | Very small \(d_0\) can generate large gain ratios                                                                                                   | Needs explicit check          |
| **Temporal/support balance**        | Equal binary coverage does not guarantee equal neighborhood quality or hour distribution                                                            | Needs CPC-blind/support audit |
| **Artifact/signal QC**              | I-CARE has no confirmed artifact-removal path; upper-tail amplification is especially sensitive                                                     | Important robustness check    |

The first one deserves emphasis.

### 1. Subject anchoring may be the biggest remaining issue

The output contract says explicitly that:

* `coords_3d_subject_anchored` is intended for **within-subject geometry, local Jacobians, trajectory shape, and reachability**;
* `coords_3d_cohort_anchored` is preferred for **clinical group comparisons**. 

But the new dynamical families—including amplification, drift, history, turning, and one-step—are currently specified as **3D subject-anchored only**. 

That creates a subtle problem.

A metric such as

$$
G_{ij}=
\frac{\|x_{i+1}-x_{j+1}\|}
{\|x_i-x_j\|}
$$

is invariant to a common scalar rescaling, but **not generally invariant to subject-specific anisotropic coordinate rescaling**.

Likewise,

$$
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(a)
=
\frac{(\operatorname{tr}a)^2}
{\operatorname{tr}(a^2)}
$$

can change under different axis-wise scaling.

So before calling the CPC contrast biologically comparable, I would do one of two things:

$$
\boxed{\text{recompute the two primary L1 quantities on cohort-anchored 3D}}
$$

as a translation/sensitivity check,

or formally demonstrate that the subject-anchor transformation used here cannot create the observed group differences.

I strongly prefer the first.

This does **not** mean replacing the current result. The local measurement should still be estimated within subjects. It means checking whether the **between-subject ordering survives a common metric frame**.

If

$$
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\uparrow,\quad G_{90}\downarrow
$$

survive cohort anchoring, I become much more confident.

---

### 2. Audit the numerical anatomy of \(G_{90}\)

Because your strongest amplification result sits in an upper quantile, I'd want to know whether it is genuine trajectory separation or denominator-floor behavior.

The metric is

$$
G_{ij}
=
\frac{d_1+\varepsilon}{d_0+\varepsilon}.
$$

I'd add a support/QC report before part2 containing at least:

$$
\operatorname{median}(d_0/\varepsilon),
$$

fraction with

$$
d_0\le 10\varepsilon,
$$

neighbor count per source, neighborhood-radius distribution, and fraction of sources whose q90 uses the minimum permissible support.

Ideally CPC1/CPC5 should not differ systematically on those support quantities.

If CPC5 simply has more extremely tiny source separations, the gain ratio could become larger without representing the phenomenon we think it does.

This isn't a reason to distrust \(G_{90}\); it's precisely the sort of audit that turns it into a strong metric.

---

### 3. Complete coverage is not quite enough—check support quality and time coverage

Your coverage audit is excellent:

$$
71/71\quad\text{vs}\quad70/70.
$$

That essentially eliminates the Family-B-style selection problem.

But I'd also compare CPC1/CPC5 on:

$$
n_{\rm transitions},\quad
n_{\rm neighbours},\quad
r_{\rm neighbourhood},\quad
\Delta t,\quad
\text{crossfit support}.
$$

And there is an especially important coma-specific issue: **time within the 0–12 h interval**.

Your earlier I-CARE work found that several geometry effects vary over time and were strongest around 3–4 h. So a 0–12 h subject median can still be confounded if CPC1 and CPC5 contribute different proportions of early versus late data.

I would therefore perform a part1-only temporal audit:

$$
\text{hourly }d_{\mathrm{eff}}
$$

and

$$
\text{hourly }G_{90},
$$

not to select the “best hour,” but to establish whether the sign is broadly stable and whether differing temporal coverage explains the aggregate.

For part2, freeze the 0–12 h aggregation now. **Do not choose a favorable hour after looking.**

If you want more temporal fairness, a reasonable frozen alternative would be:

$$
\text{median within patient-hour}
\rightarrow
\text{equal-weight median across available hours}.
$$

But decide that before opening part2.

---

### 4. Artifact robustness matters particularly for \(G_{90}\)

The guide says I-CARE's preprocessing has no confirmed artifact-reduction method, so exported `qc_ok_eeg` is typically `-1 = not assessed`, not “passed.” The conventional EEG block also warns that family-average band-power/connectivity descriptives may be outlier-heavy without artifact rejection. 

This matters more for

$$
G_{90}
$$

than for a median metric because it deliberately interrogates the upper local amplification tail.

The fact that

$$
\text{realized speed}\approx0
$$

between groups is reassuring: a simple “CPC5 EEG is noisier and everything moves faster” explanation does **not** fit your results.

But it doesn't completely eliminate episodic artifact amplification.

I'd therefore do a CPC-blind QC association:

$$
G_{90}
\sim
\text{coverage / raw amplitude / high-frequency burden / available coma-qEEG QC}.
$$

And ideally show the CPC effect persists after excluding recordings with externally defined poor signal support.

Do not tune exclusion thresholds to maximize the CPC effect.

### 5. Clarify one provenance discrepancy in the H5 guide

The guide currently says the production PhysioNet I-CARE dynamical-family overlays do **not** enable amplification/history/turning by default, with amplification named pilots described as bounded first-hour runs. Yet your 2026-09-17 run clearly contains 0–12 h L0/L1 surfaces from those families. 

That may simply mean the guide predates the latest overlay/configuration change.

But before part2 I would make sure the run manifest freezes:

* exact config filename,
* config digest,
* Git revision,
* family enable flags,
* q90 = 0.90,
* neighborhood parameters,
* distance floor \(\varepsilon\),
* coordinate layer,
* subject aggregation,
* 0–12 h timing rule.

This is a provenance/documentation issue, not a scientific objection.
