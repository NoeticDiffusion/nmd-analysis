That is a beautiful shape-versus-scale dissociation. The normalized directional entropy \(\widetilde H_a\) is naturally the entropy of the normalized covariance eigenvalues,

$$
p_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j\lambda_j},
\qquad
\widetilde H_a=
-\frac{\sum_i p_i\log p_i}{\log d},
$$

so it asks whether spread is distributed across several directions rather than concentrated into one. This is closely related to, but not identical with, participation-ratio \(d_{\rm eff}\).

# H2 may now be worth reviving

I actually think the strongest unused measure in that old run is

$$
\widetilde H_a.
$$

You currently have \(d_{\rm eff}\), but participation ratio and normalized eigen-entropy weight eigenspectrum shape differently.

For eigenvalues normalized to \(p_i\),

$$
d_{\rm eff}
=
\frac{1}{\sum_i p_i^2},
$$

while

$$
\widetilde H_a
=
-\frac{\sum_i p_i\log p_i}{\log 3}.
$$

So:

* \(d_{\rm eff}\) is more sensitive to dominant modes;
* entropy uses the full eigenvalue distribution.

This could tell us whether the cohort-frame attenuation of \(d_{\rm eff}\) reflects a real disappearance of shape richness or just sensitivity of participation ratio to axis scaling.

I would now calculate, **exploratorily on cohort-anchored re-kNN part1**:

$$
\boxed{\widetilde H_a^{\rm cohort}}

and compare it with cohort-frame \(d_{\rm eff}\).

If:

$$
d_{\rm eff}\ \text{weakens}
$$

but

$$
\widetilde H_a\ \text{remains clearly separated},

then “spread richness” has some robustness across shape metrics.

If both collapse, then the subject-frame result is more normalization-dependent than we thought.

Because you've already seen H2's outcome on part1, this cannot become a new confirmatory primary endpoint. But it is an excellent **robustness/construct-validation analysis**.
