# COMA-HORIZON-001 is now the right next experiment

This is where I think the project can move from “what is the local covariance?” toward the more NDT-specific question:

> **How does the CPC-linked future-spread geometry evolve with horizon?**

Define

$$
\Delta_h X_t=X_{t+h}-X_t
$$

and

$$
C_h(x)
=
\operatorname{Cov}
(\Delta_h X_t\mid X_t\approx x).
$$

I agree with

$$
\boxed{h\in\{2,4,8\}}
$$

especially because \(h=2\) gets you beyond the stated lag-1 raw-window overlap.

Assuming a 4-s chart step, these correspond approximately to:

$$
8\,s,\quad16\,s,\quad32\,s.
$$

I would make three summaries the core exploratory panel:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(C_h),
\qquad
d_{\rm eff}(C_h),
\qquad
\widetilde H(C_h).
$$

No Jacobian propagation and no generator.

## One design choice matters a lot: support

Different \(h\) automatically loses different amounts of data near run ends and gaps. If you simply compute each horizon on everything available, apparent horizon changes could partly be support changes.

I would therefore freeze two analyses:

**Primary: common-support trajectory.**

A source point counts only if it has valid successors at **all three** horizons:

$$
t+2,\quad t+4,\quad t+8.
$$

Then the same source population produces \(C_2,C_4,C_8\).

That gives the cleanest statement about horizon.

**Sensitivity: horizon-maximal support.**

Allow every horizon to use all its valid source points.

If both tell the same story, excellent. If not, support attrition itself is informative.

Neighbors should be selected from the **source states only**, in the frozen cohort frame, and then the same source neighbors followed to \(t+h\). Never re-neighbor at the destination.

---

# I would also keep scale and shape separate over horizon

For each horizon we get:

$$
S_h=\operatorname{Tr}(C_h)
$$

and

$$
D_h=d_{\rm eff}(C_h),
\qquad
H_h=\widetilde H(C_h).
$$

Then ask two distinct questions.

**Magnitude growth**

$$
S_2\rightarrow S_4\rightarrow S_8.
$$

Does CPC5's larger spread magnitude persist, increase, or converge?

And separately **shape evolution**

$$
D_2\rightarrow D_4\rightarrow D_8.
$$

Does CPC1 develop relatively richer finite-horizon geometry even though its total spread is smaller?

That could generate a genuinely interesting pattern such as:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(C_h)_{\rm CPC1}
<
\operatorname{Tr}(C_h)_{\rm CPC5}
$$

while

$$
d_{\rm eff}(C_h)_{\rm CPC1}
>
d_{\rm eff}(C_h)_{\rm CPC5}.
$$

That would be a much stronger finite-horizon version of the current **scale–shape dissociation**.

Or the shape difference may disappear entirely after \(h=2\), which would tell us it is very local.

Either result matters.

### I would avoid immediately normalizing by horizon

For a Brownian-like process one might look at

$$
C_h/(h\Delta t),
$$

but doing that immediately risks importing a diffusion interpretation.

Use raw

$$
C_h
$$

as primary finite-horizon increment geometry.

A horizon-normalized trace can be a secondary diagnostic:

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(C_h)}{h\Delta t},
$$

but call it a **finite-horizon spread rate**, not a diffusion coefficient.

# An especially interesting derived object comes almost for free

Without inventing a new CPC scalar, we can characterize how rapidly spread grows:

$$
\beta_S
=
\frac{
\log\operatorname{Tr}(C_8)
-
\log\operatorname{Tr}(C_2)
}{
\log 8-\log2
}.
$$

This resembles a scaling exponent:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(C_h)\propto h^{\beta_S}.
$$

But I would keep it **exploratory only** on part1.

Why it is interesting:

* \(\beta\approx1\): roughly linear variance growth;
* \(\beta<1\): sublinear/constrained spread;
* \(\beta>1\): superlinear finite-horizon growth.

That could eventually connect NDT to a much deeper question than CPC classification: whether different neural regimes have different **future-spread scaling laws**.

But first inspect the actual \(h=2,4,8\) curves. Do not lead with the exponent.
