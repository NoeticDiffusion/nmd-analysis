Same dataset, so not independent replication—but it is mechanistically informative convergence.

d(g_{90}^{\rm obs})<0

It says the CPC difference is **not represented by the maximal gain of the qualified one-step linear map**.

And in the same subject frame:

$$
\Delta g
=
g_{90}^{\rm obs}
-
g_{\max}^{\rm op}
$$

separates:

$$

So the result is better expressed as:

$$
\boxed{
\text{CPC5 has greater observed amplification beyond what the fitted linear one-step operator captures.}
}

But I would keep exactly your current term:

> **model gap**

because its cause is unidentified.

It could reflect any mixture of:

* nonlinear local dynamics,
* state/history dependence,
* stochastic fluctuations,
* latent variables,
* nonstationarity,
* observation/chart effects,
* residual artifact.

It is **not** diffusion and not evidence for any one of those mechanisms.

The mixed-frame \(\Delta g\) should remain essentially diagnostic only. Since \(\Phi\) is subject-anchored, the scientifically coherent model-gap result is the same-frame one.

# There is one thing I would test next

Your sentence

> **CPC5 is less stable, not just shifted**

is highly plausible now, but I would add one final falsification test before elevating “instability” into a major paper claim.

MAD often grows with the central level of a positive-valued quantity. Since CPC5 also has higher \(G_{90}\) and \(R_{b/a}\), some larger absolute MAD can arise mechanically from that shift.

So I would run:

$$
\boxed{\text{COMA-STAB-ADJ-001}}
$$

with the question:

> Does CPC5 retain greater between-run dispersion after accounting for its higher central level?

For each patient and metric \(M\), you already have

$$
\tilde M_i
=
\operatorname{median}_r(M_{ir})
$$

and

$$
S_i
=
MAD_r(M_{ir}).
$$

Test something like:

$$
\log(S_i+\epsilon)
=
\beta_0
+
\beta_1\log(\tilde M_i+\epsilon)
+
\beta_2\,I(CPC1)
+
\beta_3 n_{\rm runs}
+\cdots
$$

with the adjustment structure frozen before outcome inspection if you add site or other covariates.

The crucial coefficient is

$$
\beta_2.
$$

If CPC1 still has lower dispersion after adjusting for level, then “less stable, not merely shifted” becomes much stronger.

An even simpler sensitivity is a robust relative dispersion:

$$
\boxed{
RD
=
\frac{MAD(M)}
{\operatorname{median}(M)}
}
$$

for positive well-separated-from-zero metrics.

I'd prefer the level-adjusted analysis as primary and `MAD/median` as sensitivity rather than vice versa.

Either outcome teaches us something.
