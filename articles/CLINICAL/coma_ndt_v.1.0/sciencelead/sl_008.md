Yes. I think we now have enough structure to design a **coma-specific Level-1 experiment** that is more informative than simply searching for another CPC discriminator.

I would call it:

## COMA-DDC-001 — Drift–Spread Coupling

The question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Does recovery preserve conditional spread \emph{across} the local mean-flow direction,
rather than merely along it?}
}
$$

This directly develops the pattern already emerging in part1: CPC1 has broader conditional increment-covariance geometry, is not moving faster, and has less extreme amplification. NDT explicitly treats deterministic drift and stochastic spread as distinct components of the future law, so their *relationship* is a legitimate new object rather than another version of spectral abscissa. 

One important contract point first: the existing H5 `A_bD` and `R_b_over_a` fields cannot simply be used. They are currently `not_testable` because ingest was not supplied an independent drift estimate, and the current chart-drift family is explicitly not wired in as that independent source.  So I would construct this **analysis-side with blocked cross-fitting**, and not claim it is the existing ingest `A_bD`/`R_b_over_a`.

### The local objects

At each cohort-anchored query state \(x\), estimate the finite-lag conditional mean

$$
\hat b_\Delta(x)
=
\frac{E[\Delta X\mid X_t\approx x]}{\Delta t}
$$

and conditional increment covariance

$$
\hat a_\Delta(x)
=
\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(\Delta X\mid X_t\approx x)}{\Delta t}.
$$

These are still Level 1. In particular, \(\hat a_\Delta\) is **raw centered conditional increment covariance**, not an Itô diffusion tensor or residual innovation covariance. 

For the experiment I would estimate them from disjoint temporal reference blocks:

$$
(b_A,a_B),\qquad (b_B,a_A),
$$

using the same two chronological folds and embargo logic as the existing crossfit path. Calculate every metric in both directions and average the two. This gives us genuine cross-fitted evidence coupling rather than using the same increments to define both vectors.

### Metric 1 — conditional-mean-aligned spread fraction

Let

$$
u_b=\frac{\hat b}{\|\hat b\|}.
$$

Then define

$$
\boxed{
F_{\parallel}
=
\frac{
u_b^\top \hat a\,u_b
}{
\operatorname{tr}(\hat a)
}
}
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{
F_{\perp}=1-F_{\parallel}.
}
$$

I would name the exported analysis identities:

`conditional_mean_aligned_spread_fraction_level1`

and

`conditional_mean_orthogonal_spread_fraction_level1`.

Interpretation is unusually simple. \(F_\parallel\) asks how much of the conditional spread lies along the direction the local mean trajectory is trying to move; \(F_\perp\) asks how much lies outside that corridor.

In three dimensions, isotropic spread gives

$$
F_\parallel=\frac13,\qquad F_\perp=\frac23.
$$

So this even has a meaningful geometric reference point.

My **prospective coma hypothesis** would be:

$$
\boxed{
F_{\perp,\mathrm{CPC1}}
>
F_{\perp,\mathrm{CPC5}}.
}
$$

That would mean CPC1 retains more locally available variation transverse to current mean flow—something close to “more ways out of the current corridor,” without calling it consciousness or controllability.

### Metric 2 — drift-to-spread ratio

We also want to know whether the local dynamics are mostly carried by systematic mean motion or by conditional spread.

A dimensionless comparison is

$$
\boxed{
R_{b/a}
=
\frac{
\Delta t\,\|\hat b\|^2
}{
\operatorname{tr}(\hat a)
}.
}
$$

The dimensional reasoning is useful:

$$
\|\hat b\Delta t\|^2
=
\|\hat b\|^2\Delta t^2
$$

is squared mean displacement over one step, while

$$
\operatorname{tr}(\hat a)\Delta t
$$

is total conditional mean-square spread over that interval. Their ratio is exactly the expression above.

I would call it:

`conditional_mean_to_spread_ratio_level1`.

Interpretation:

$$
R_{b/a}\gg1
$$

means strongly transport-dominated local evolution;

$$
R_{b/a}\ll1
$$

means conditional spread dominates over systematic displacement.

Given the part1 observations so far, my **prospective hypothesis** would be

$$
\boxed{
R_{b/a,\mathrm{CPC1}}
<
R_{b/a,\mathrm{CPC5}}.
}
$$

Not because CPC1 necessarily has “more noise,” but because it may combine relatively weak mean-channel dominance with a broader spread geometry.

This is close in spirit to the old transport–innovation question, but it remains strictly at the finite-lag chart level.

### Metric 3 — orthogonal spread dimensionality

This is the one I think may yield the most *new* information.

Project the covariance into the plane perpendicular to drift:

$$
P_\perp
=
I-u_bu_b^\top
$$

and

$$
a_\perp
=
P_\perp \hat a P_\perp.
$$

Then define

$$
\boxed{
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}
=
\frac{
[\operatorname{tr}(a_\perp)]^2
}{
\operatorname{tr}(a_\perp^2)
}.
}
$$

In a 3D chart:

$$
1\le d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}\le2
$$

when transverse spread is nonzero.

This distinguishes two very different geometries:

$$
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}\approx1
$$

means that even the “escape” from drift is essentially one-dimensional;

whereas

$$
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}\approx2
$$

means the system has an approximately two-dimensional transverse sheet of local possibilities.

Name:

`orthogonal_spread_effective_dimension_level1`.

Prospective hypothesis:

$$
\boxed{
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff,CPC1}}
>
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff,CPC5}}.
}
$$

This could tell us *why* the total \(d_{\rm eff}\) is larger in CPC1.

## One implementation detail is critical

Do **not** join the currently stored `b_hat` and `a_hat` arrays by row position.

The H5 guide explicitly says the diffusion `source_idx` identifies lag-1 source transitions and is not aligned one-to-one with the drift arrays/neighborhoods. 

The implementer should instead:

1. read the cohort-anchored coordinate values;
2. build the same eligible lag-1 source set;
3. make two chronological reference folds with the frozen embargo;
4. estimate \(b_A(x)\) and \(a_A(x)\) using the registered L1 estimators;
5. independently estimate \(b_B(x)\) and \(a_B(x)\);
6. compute \(F_\perp(b_A,a_B)\), \(R(b_A,a_B)\), \(d_{\perp,\rm eff}(b_A,a_B)\);
7. swap A/B and average;
8. then take the recording/window series → patient 0–12 h median.

If \(\|\hat b\|\) is numerically zero, the directional metrics are **undefined/NaN**, not zero. `conditional_mean_to_spread_ratio_level1` can still be zero in that case because its meaning remains defined.

## Before CPC labels: a very small truth-known gate

I would require this new estimator to pass four synthetic cases:

$$
a=\sigma^2I
$$

must yield

$$
F_\perp\approx\frac23.
$$

A diffusion tensor elongated **along** \(b\) must reduce \(F_\perp\).

A tensor elongated **orthogonal** to \(b\) must increase \(F_\perp\).

And a two-dimensional isotropic transverse plane must give

$$
d_{\perp,\rm eff}\approx2,
$$

whereas a single transverse channel must approach 1.

Also check invariance to a **common rotation and scalar rescaling** of the chart. Do not require invariance to arbitrary anisotropic rescaling—that is exactly why we've now frozen the common cohort frame.

## Why this test is interesting if it works

Suppose part1 eventually gives:

$$
F_\perp\uparrow,
\qquad
d_{\perp,\mathrm{eff}}\uparrow,
\qquad
R_{b/a}\downarrow
$$

in CPC1.

Combined with the already observed

$$
d_{\rm eff}^{(a)}\uparrow,
\qquad
G_{90}\downarrow,
\qquad
\text{speed}\approx\text{same},
$$

the phenotype becomes much more specific:

$$
\boxed{
\text{CPC1:
broader and more transverse conditional local freedom,
but less extreme amplification and no faster overall motion.}
}
$$

Whereas CPC5 would look more like:

$$
\boxed{
\text{more channelized / drift-coupled local evolution,
with stronger upper-tail amplification.}
}
$$

That would be a very interesting **Plausible Interpretation** of recovery-linked geometry—not yet a mechanism.

And, importantly, it cannot be reduced to the existing \(d_{\rm eff}\) result. Two patients can have the same total covariance eigenvalues and therefore identical \(d_{\rm eff}\), yet differ completely in whether those directions lie **along or across the local mean drift**. That is the genuinely new scientific information COMA-DDC-001 would add.

I would explicitly avoid building a single “coma score” such as \(F_\perp d_{\perp,\rm eff}/(1+R)\) at this stage. That would be too easy to optimize against CPC1/CPC5. First establish that the separate geometric components behave as predicted; a composite can come later if an independent cohort justifies it.
